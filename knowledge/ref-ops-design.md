# Trial Design Chain — Core Decisions (workflow B)


## 1. Trial design chain (workflow B)

Evidence-production chain: `development objective → clinical question → protocol design → subject protection → executable process → data generation → statistical analysis → medical interpretation → study report → registration decision`. Track upstream–downstream impact for any local decision (an endpoint change ripples to timeline, CRF, randomization, SAP, sample size, TFL, CSR, subject burden).

### 1.1 Shared decision loop
1. Define the decision (interpret / choose / execute / correct / review / write); 2. Build the problem profile (product, indication, phase, design, jurisdiction, role, document & data status); 3. Identify the key objective or risk (subject protection / result reliability / registration use / operational feasibility); 4. Determine the evidence needed (what is confirmed, what must be officially or project-supplemented); 5. Design controls (prevent / detect / escalate / record); 6. Specify decision rules (who, under what condition, continue / suspend / modify / close); 7. Close the loop (owner, time point, evidence, approval, verification, cross-file update). When a key fact changes the conclusion, clarify first, then judge.

### 1.2 Design choices (compare by fit, not template) Parallel / crossover / single-arm / randomized / open / blinded / superiority / non-inferiority — compare on: fit to the clinical question, bias control, ethical acceptability, subject burden, sample size & time, operational feasibility, interpretability & registration use. Randomization balances known & unknown prognostic factors but cannot eliminate selection / implementation / measurement / loss-to-follow-up / reporting bias; when blinding is impossible, strengthen independent assessment, objective endpoints, standardized procedures and blinded data review.

### 1.3 Inclusion / exclusion, endpoints, comparators
- Each inclusion / exclusion criterion serves at least one purpose (protect subjects / reduce confounding / ensure endpoint assessment / implement risk control / define population); prefer enhanced monitoring, stabilization periods, stratification, rescue over direct exclusion; criteria must be objective & executable (history / time window / exam / threshold).
- Endpoints must satisfy clinical relevance, measurement reliability, reasonable timing, executability, statistical tractability. Primary / secondary / exploratory hierarchy must align with study objective, multiplicity, and reporting claims; composite / surrogate / PRO / biomarker endpoints need explicit composition, determination, missing-data handling and clinical interpretation.
- Comparator chosen by clinical question, ethics, assay sensitivity, interpretability: placebo / standard-of-care / active / dose / external control; historical or external controls must control for time trends, selection and measurement differences.

### 1.4 ct-series integration anchor (layer B)
- **Sample-size handoff**: this skill gives the parameter framework in workflow `C` (assumptions / effect size / variability / alpha / power / allocation ratio / dropout); once complete, hand off to `ct-samplesize` for actual computation — **do not compute n in-house** (see `scripts/workflows.json` `integration.sample_size_handoff`).
- **Data grounding**: when advice needs real-data support, read sibling-skill outputs per `scripts/workflows.json` `integration.data_grounding` — `ct-registry` (peer-trial count / phase / region to argue feasibility), `ct-safety` (FAERS signals to support AE monitoring), `ct-literature` (published evidence for endpoint precedent); a competitive-landscape view is stitched in-house from the three data skills — and explicitly label "Data source: ct-xxx on <date>". Pure methodology questions with no data need may skip this and state "no data grounding performed".

# Early Phase, Clinical Pharmacology & Special Populations (workflow B/C/D)


### 1.5 Early-phase & clinical pharmacology (B)
- BA / BE: choose crossover or parallel by half-life / carryover / within-subject variability / safety; washout controls parent drug, metabolite, pharmacodynamic residual; sampling covers absorption peak and elimination phase; predefine pre-dose concentration, vomiting, missed sampling, aberrant dosing, concomitant meds, analyte, PK parameters, determination and unevaluable rules. Food-effect studies standardize meal type / timing / dosing time / water / meal completion.
- FIH / dose escalation: starting dose grounded in toxicology / pharmacology / target / exposure prediction / uncertainty; risk control includes sentinel dosing, cohort staggered enrollment, observation intervals, cumulative review; predefine individual / cohort / study-level hold, de-escalation, escalation and termination criteria.**Dose-finding strategy for a combination-drug IND**: For a novel-target drug A combined with marketed drug B under a combination IND: ① **Starting-dose basis**: A's monotherapy PK/PD (exposure–response), mechanistic synergy evidence (e.g. A sensitizes B), A's monotherapy MTD/RP2D (if available); B's marketed dose and exposure; the combination starting dose typically takes "a fraction of or below A's monotherapy RP2D + B's standard dose" to avoid additive unknown toxicity; ② **Expected DDI**: assess A's effect on B's metabolism/transporters (method in §1.6) and B's effect on A, and preset dose-adjustment rules; ③ **Early stopping rules**: based on dose-limiting toxicity (DLT), additive toxicity, and premature pharmacodynamic saturation, preset cohort-hold / dose-de-escalation / termination rules; ④ combination vs sequential, and whether to include a monotherapy control arm, must be justified in the protocol. Combination starting dose, DDI, and stopping-rule regulatory expectations are dynamic items — follow current NMPA/FDA IND and combination-drug guidance (officially verify).
- PopPK: define the model-supported decision first, then design sampling; evaluate stability, predictive performance, mechanistic plausibility, covariate clinical impact and interpretability of dose recommendation.
- DDI: from metabolic enzymes / transporters / in-vitro / clinical PK / target-population concomitant meds, decide clinical study, opportunistic assessment or model prediction; translate results into contraindications / restrictions, dose adjustment, monitoring and labeling.

### 1.6 Clinical pharmacology DDI decision: R-value, positive criteria, and labeling uncertainty (workflow C / B) DDI decisions must be quantified: reversible inhibition uses **R = 1 + [I]/Ki** ([I] takes the most conservative steady-state peak concentration, free or total depending on mechanism); the FDA in vitro DDI guidance typically sets the positive criterion for reversible inhibition at **R ≥ 1.1**; R = 1.2 already exceeds the threshold, and under the static [I]/Ki method a formal clinical DDI study is generally warranted. Notes: ① use the most conservative [I] (total Cmax or free Cmax, depending on the inhibition mechanism); ② **the transporter dimension is not counted in the CYP R-value** — if the drug is also an OATP1B1 (etc.) transporter substrate with a narrow therapeutic index, even a borderline CYP R-value leaves transporter-mediated interaction concerns (e.g. elevated statin levels) that must be assessed separately; ③ **conditions to skip the DDI study**: only when the R-value is below threshold **and** there is no narrow therapeutic window / no sensitive transporter substrate / no mechanistic concern may the uncertainty be honestly described in the label and the study skipped; ④ **labeling uncertainty wording**: if skipped, state "CYP3A4 inhibition suggested (predicted R = 1.2)", "OATP1B1 substrate — avoid co-administration with drugs sharing this transporter substrate property or intensify monitoring", "no clinical DDI study completed; interaction potential not fully characterized" — do not overstate safety. Current FDA《In Vitro / Clinical Drug Interaction Studies》thresholds, R-value conventions, and OATP criteria are dynamic items — follow the official text (officially verify).

### 1.7 Pediatric population dose adjustment and Assent age stratification (workflow B / D) — weight/BSA individualized dosing and age-stratified assent
- **Context / Role**: Pediatric specialist / clinical pharmacologist — pediatric trials must adjust dose by weight/BSA, and obtain Assent from maturity-capable children by age stratum, with the legal guardian signing informed consent.
- **Correct handling path**: ① **Dose adjustment**: pediatric dose is commonly individualized by weight (mg/kg) or body surface area (BSA); predefine dose-calculation rules, rounding/caps, overweight/renal-impairment adjustment, and recalculation triggers; ② **Assent age stratification**: stratify children by maturity (e.g. ≤6 years: guardian consent only; 7–11 years: simplified Assent; ≥12 years: written Assent), with different language and form per stratum; ③ **child refusal takes precedence**: a child's explicit refusal of invasive procedures / continued participation takes precedence over the guardian's request (see §3.5.1); ④ **guardian consent**: the legal guardian signs informed consent, a separate document from Assent; ⑤ **ethics and safety**: pediatric trials require additional ethics review (minimal risk, direct benefit, risk–benefit), with more sensitive AE monitoring.
- **Key points**: Pediatric dosing must be individualized and the dose calculation verifiable; Assent is an age-stratified ongoing process, not a one-time event.
- ⚠️ Pediatric dose formulas, Assent age thresholds, and pediatric ethics-review requirements are dynamic items — follow 《儿科人群药物临床试验技术指导原则》 and current GCP (officially verify).

### 1.8 Model-guided dose exploration (CRM / BOIN) (workflow B) — model-based dose escalation replacing 3+3
- **Context / Role**: Clinical pharmacologist — FIH dose exploration uses CRM (Continual Reassessment Method) or BOIN (Bayesian Optimal Interval), setting the target toxicity probability and dose-escalation / hold / de-escalation decisions.
- **Correct handling path**: ① **Target toxicity probability**: preset an acceptable DLT rate (e.g. 25%–30%); CRM/BOIN then allocates subjects dynamically to doses near the MTD; ② **BOIN**: interval-based simple Bayesian; each dose cohort decides by preset escalation/de-escalation boundaries — transparent and easy to explain to the IRB; ③ **CRM**: model-based update of the dose–toxicity curve, requiring robust priors and frequent model checks; ④ compared with the §1.5 traditional cohort escalation: model-based methods are more efficient but need stricter simulation and monitoring; ⑤ preset hold/termination rules with DSMB/iDMC oversight (§3.7).
- **Key points**: Model-guided methods improve MTD estimation efficiency but require preset target toxicity rate, priors, and stopping rules validated by simulation.
- ⚠️ Target toxicity rate, CRM/BOIN implementation, and regulatory acceptance are dynamic items — follow current FDA/EMA/NMPA FIH and dose-finding guidance (officially verify).

### 1.9 Hepatic / renal impairment population dose adjustment (workflow B / C) — starting dose and adjustment under organ-function impairment
- **Context / Role**: Clinical pharmacologist / biostatistician — how to determine starting dose and dose adjustment for subjects with hepatic / renal insufficiency.
- **Correct handling path**: ① **Clearance pathway first**: first identify the drug's main clearance pathway (hepatic metabolism / renal excretion); hepatic impairment affects metabolic enzymes and transporters, renal impairment affects parent-drug excretion; ② **starting-dose adjustment**: based on popPK / population PK and intrinsic factors (organ function) modeling, preset dose coefficients or prohibition strata per hepatic-impairment (e.g. Child-Pugh grade) / renal-impairment (eGFR/CrCl grade) level; ③ **trial design**: the protocol pre-specifies organ-function-stratified inclusion/exclusion and a dose-adjustment table; severe impairment is usually excluded or assigned a dedicated low-dose cohort; ④ **dynamic monitoring**: subjects with dynamically changing hepatic/renal function must be re-evaluated for dose; ⑤ **label linkage**: hepatic/renal impairment doses must be stated clearly in the IB/label (linking to §1.5 starting dose).
- **Key points**: Organ-impairment dosing must be based on clearance mechanism and exposure–response modeling, not empirical reduction; stratification and the adjustment table must be pre-written into the protocol.
- ⚠️ Hepatic/renal impairment grading criteria, dose-adjustment coefficients, and exclusion criteria are dynamic items — follow current FDA/EMA/NMPA guidance on organ-impairment populations and PopPK (officially verify).

### 1.10 Quantitative pharmacology and exposure–response modeling (workflow B / C) — PopPK / PK-PD guiding dose and dosing regimen
- **Context / Role**: Clinical pharmacologist — use population PK (PopPK) and PK-PD models to quantify the exposure–response relationship, guiding dose selection, dosing interval, and special-population adjustment.
- **Correct handling path**: ① **Model-guided**: build PopPK/PK-PD from sparse sampling (§1.5) to quantify covariate (weight, renal impairment, concomitant medication as in §1.6) effects on exposure; ② **exposure–response**: link exposure to efficacy/safety endpoints, determining the target exposure range and optimal dose; ③ **dose decision**: provide the basis for starting dose (§1.5), organ-function adjustment (§1.9), and dosing interval; ④ **simulation**: use the model to simulate the power and safety of different regimens, supporting protocol design and regulatory communication; ⑤ **validation**: the model is internally/externally validated, with transparent uncertainty.
- **Key points**: Quantitative pharmacology turns "dose" from empirical to exposure–response driven; the model must be validated and its uncertainty transparent.
- ⚠️ Quantitative-pharmacology models, exposure–response relationships, and dose-optimization methods are dynamic items — follow current FDA/EMA/NMPA guidance on population PK and model-informed drug development (officially verify).

### 1.11 Special considerations for geriatric-population trials (workflow B / D) — multimorbidity, polypharmacy, and cognitive consent
- **Context / Role**: Clinical pharmacology / investigator — elderly subjects (often multimorbid, polypharmacy, high renal-impairment prevalence) enrolling requires special design and protection.
- **Correct handling path**: ① **Inclusion/exclusion**: avoid overly strict exclusion that harms external validity; pre-specify a geriatric subgroup and comorbidity stratification; ② **polypharmacy**: polypharmacy is common in the elderly, raising concomitant-medication (§4.8) and DDI (§1.6) risk — strengthen monitoring; ③ **high renal-impairment prevalence**: elderly have a high renal-impairment proportion, so dose must be adjusted per §1.9; ④ **cognition and consent**: cognitively declining subjects need a legal representative + the subject's own wishes (see §2.2 / §3.5.1), analogous to Assent; ⑤ **endpoints**: geriatric-appropriate endpoints (function, falls, cognition) and tolerability considerations.
- **Key points**: Geriatric trials must balance "representativeness" and "vulnerable-protection" — stratified design + polypharmacy/renal-impairment management + consent-capacity assessment.
- ⚠️ Geriatric-population inclusion/exclusion, dosing, and consent-capacity assessment requirements are dynamic items — follow current GCP and geriatric-pharmacy / geriatric-medicine guidance (officially verify).

### 1.12 Pharmacogenomics (PGx) and dose guidance (workflow B / C) — genetic polymorphisms affecting exposure and response
- **Context / Role**: Clinical pharmacology — drug-metabolizing-enzyme / transporter genetic polymorphisms (e.g. CYP2C19, DPYD, HLA) affect exposure or serious adverse reactions and must be incorporated into dose and monitoring strategy.
- **Correct handling path**: ① **Genotype–phenotype**: clarify the polymorphism's effect on metabolism (poor/extensive metabolizer), transport, and immunity (e.g. HLA-B*1502 and SJS); ② **dose/selection**: adjust starting dose by genotype, select an alternative drug, or contraindicate (e.g. reduce fluorouracil dose in DPYD deficiency); ③ **test timing**: pre-dose genetic testing must be pre-specified and written into the protocol/IB to avoid post-hoc stratification; ④ **joint consideration**: assess jointly with §1.6 DDI and §1.9 organ impairment; ⑤ **distinguish from companion diagnostics**: PGx is for dose/safety, while companion diagnostics (§3.7) are for enrichment/approval.
- **Key points**: PGx turns "individual variation" from empirical warning to gene-traceable evidence — must be pre-specified, written into the protocol, and linked to safety monitoring.
- ⚠️ PGx testing indications, dose adjustment, and contraindication requirements are dynamic items — follow current FDA/EMA/NMPA pharmacogenomics and labeling guidance (officially verify).

### 1.13 Drug abuse potential and dependence assessment (workflow B / C) — pre-requisite safety evidence for CNS-active drugs
- **Context / Role**: Clinical pharmacology / safety — central nervous system (CNS)-active drugs must be assessed early in development for abuse potential and physical/psychological dependence, to guide labeling and control.
- **Correct handling path**: ① **Risk prediction**: predict abuse-related risk by pharmacological class (stimulant/depressant/opioid/cannabinoid, etc.); ② **nonclinical**: in vitro receptor/transporter binding, animal drug-discrimination/self-administration/physical-dependence (withdrawal) studies; ③ **human**: human abuse-potential study (vs a known abusable drug, placebo-controlled); ④ **mitigation**: abuse-deterrent formulations, dispensing restrictions, REMS/risk plan (§4.9 RMP); ⑤ **labeling/control**: affects the label "pharmacological dependence" item and controlled-substance scheduling.
- **Key points**: Abuse-potential assessment is the "pre-requisite safety evidence" for CNS drugs — three tiers of in vitro/animal/human evidence + abuse-deterrent formulation as a fallback.
- ⚠️ Abuse-potential study design, abuse-deterrent formulation standards, and controlled-substance scheduling are dynamic items — follow current FDA/EMA/NMPA dependence-assessment and controlled-substance regulations (officially verify).

### 1.14 Photosafety (phototoxicity / photoallergy) assessment (workflow B / C) — nonclinical-to-clinical chain of phototoxicity
- **Context / Role**: Clinical pharmacology / nonclinical — drugs with light-absorbing properties (systemic/topical exposure, dermal application, some anti-infective/psychotropic/diuretic/NSAID drugs) must be assessed for phototoxicity and photoallergy.
- **Correct handling path**: ① **Nonclinical**: 3T3 NRU phototoxicity assay, photomutagenicity/photocarcinogenicity assessment (structural alerts); ② **clinical**: early phototoxicity clinical trial (dosing + irradiation), symptom monitoring (erythema/edema/pigmentation); ③ **risk stratification**: decide clinical and label warnings based on exposure site, absorption wavelength, and half-life; ④ **notification**: sun-protection/light-avoidance guidance written into the ICF (§2.1)/IB; ⑤ **linkage**: photocarcinogenicity signals link to §5.25 carcinogenicity assessment.
- **Key points**: Photosafety is a "nonclinical screening → clinical validation → label warning" chain — structural alerts + wavelength/exposure drive the decision.
- ⚠️ Photosafety test methods, judgment criteria, and label-warning requirements are dynamic items — follow ICH S10 and current regulatory photosafety guidance (officially verify).

### 1.15 Pediatric formulation, palatability / dosing device (workflow B / D) — acceptable + precisely divisible dose
- **Context / Role**: Clinical pharmacology / pediatrics — pediatric trials must consider formulation palatability, swallowing ability, dosing device, and strength allowing precise weight-based dose division.
- **Correct handling path**: ① **Formulation selection**: liquid / granules / oral suspension / divisible tablets; avoid directly splitting adult formulations; ② **palatability**: taste-masking/flavoring, color/smell acceptability, to avoid difficult administration causing poor compliance; ③ **device**: measuring cup / dropper / oral syringe for precise dosing, training caregivers; ④ **dose link**: individualize by weight/BSA (§1.7 age stratification); ⑤ **link with Assent (§1.7) / pediatric ethics**.
- **Key points**: Pediatric formulation is "acceptable + precisely divisible" — palatability and device directly affect compliance and accurate dosing.
- ⚠️ Pediatric formulation, palatability, and dosing-device requirements are dynamic items — follow current pediatric-formulation and GCP guidance (officially verify).

### 1.16 Cardiovascular safety and QTc / thorough QT assessment (workflow B / C) — nonclinical hERG → clinical TQT → label
- **Context / Role**: Clinical pharmacology / safety — drugs with delayed ventricular repolarization risk (especially hERG inhibition, psychotropic/anti-infective/antiarrhythmic classes) must be assessed for QT prolongation and arrhythmia risk.
- **Correct handling path**: ① **Nonclinical**: hERG channel assay, animal telemetry QT; ② **clinical**: comprehensive QT (cQT) / thorough QT (TQT) study, concentration–QT (cQT) analysis; ③ **monitoring**: electrocardiogram (ECG) QTc periodic monitoring, electrolyte correction; ④ **risk stratification**: define label warnings based on TQT result and exposure (§2.1); ⑤ **dose link**: affects recommended dose and safety margin (§1.5).
- **Key points**: QTc safety is a "nonclinical hERG → clinical TQT → label warning" chain — concentration–QT analysis supports risk stratification.
- ⚠️ QTc/TQT study design, judgment, and ECG monitoring requirements are dynamic items — follow ICH E14/S7B and current regulatory cardiac-safety guidance (officially verify).

### 1.17 Bioanalytical and sample-analysis (PK / BA) method validation (workflow B / C) — the data foundation for PK / BE
- **Context / Role**: Clinical pharmacology / bioanalysis — sample analysis supporting PK/exposure–response (§1.10) and BE (§5.20) requires a validated bioanalytical method (LC-MS/MS, etc.).
- **Correct handling path**: ① **Method validation**: specificity/selectivity, accuracy/precision, recovery, matrix effect, stability, lower limit of quantification LLOQ; ② **sample management**: collection/processing/storage/transport temperature and timeliness (§4.1.3 sample chain); ③ **batch QC**: concurrent QC samples, calibration curve, re-assay rules; ④ **PK/BA link**: data support exposure–response and BE determination; ⑤ **GLP**: bioanalysis often requires GLP compliance.
- **Key points**: Bioanalysis is the "data foundation for PK/BE" — three pillars: method validation + sample chain + batch QC.
- ⚠️ Bioanalytical method validation, sample handling, and GLP requirements are dynamic items — follow FDA bioanalytical method validation and current regulatory guidance (officially verify).
