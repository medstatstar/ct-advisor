# Records, Source Data & Data Chain (workflow G · 4.1-4.4)


## 4. Documents, data & e-systems (workflow G · execution layer)

> The CSR **regulatory structure** (E3 main chain, consistency chain, submission-level quality gate) is in `ref-reg-submission.md` §5; this section covers execution.

### 4.1 Required records & data chain

Records / data must reconstruct "what was done, why, by whom, when, with what result", with ALCOA+ attributes (attributable / legible / contemporaneous / original / accurate / complete / consistent / enduring / available). eTMF is the ongoing evidence system proving how the trial was designed, approved, executed, monitored and closed — not a repository uploaded only at the end. Data chain: `source record → CRF / external data → cleaning & coding → reconciliation → analysis dataset → TFL → CSR`; each transformation is traceable, explainable, reproducible; corrections do not overwrite original values or break the audit trail.

**Subject diaries / paper scales as source data**: A subject-completed pain NRS (0–10) diary is **source data** and must meet ALCOA+; when the CRC transcribes it into the EDC, the **original paper record must be retained** (not destroyed or replaced by the transcribed value alone) — the original and the transcription together form the traceable evidence chain (transcription discrepancies are handled per the §3.4 discrepancy loop); ePRO electronic capture may replace paper but must be validated, time-stamped and signed. The scope of source data and transcription retention are dynamic items, subject to current GCP and data standards (verify officially).

### 4.1.1 Investigator Site File (ISF) retention & custody (workflow D / QA)

The ISF is the site-level essential document file; the retention period is a dynamic item subject to current GCP / regulations and contract (commonly several years after trial completion; the specific duration and start point must be verified against official requirements). Where the institution lacks long-term storage, the sponsor may consolidate and archive under the agreement / quality agreement, but the investigator remains primarily responsible for accessibility and authenticity of essential documents — a clear transfer list, custody responsibility, and retrieval / destruction process must be defined. eTMF (sponsor) and ISF (site) serve different purposes; both must be ALCOA+ and traceable (see §4.1 data chain). **Distinguishing the subject's original medical records (source documents) from the ISF**: original medical records are essential source documents with similar or longer retention; both must be retained per the trial document retention obligation. When the hospital's routine record destruction cycle (e.g. >5 years) precedes the trial document retention period, the investigator should: ① trial-related medical records / ISF are bound by GCP and contract, the retention usually exceeds the hospital routine destruction cycle, and must not unconditionally follow hospital destruction; ② before destruction, transfer essential documents (including copies of original medical records / ISF) to the sponsor for consolidated archiving per contract / quality agreement (allowed when the institution lacks long-term storage, see above); ③ define a transfer list, custody responsibility and retrieval / destruction process, leaving an audit trail. The specific retention duration and start point are dynamic items, subject to current GCP / regulations and contract (verify officially).

**Screen-failure subject archiving**: Subjects who, after signing the ICF, fail screening (e.g. lab out of range) and are not enrolled must still be assigned a **screening ID** in the EDC / IXRS and flagged as screen-failure — the screening ID is the key identifier for tracking subjects who "were exposed to this trial", supporting safety follow-up, withdrawal queries and regulatory inspection; their signed ICF (with version, date), lab reports etc. are archived as **essential documents** in the ISF / eTMF or a dedicated screening file, retained for the same period as the trial document obligation. The scope of screening-ID assignment and screening-file retention are dynamic items, subject to protocol / SOP and current GCP (verify officially).

### 4.1.2 ALCOA+ compliance of decentralized trial (DCT) data flows (workflow E / D)

DCT introduces non-research-team members (e.g. home-nurse visits) and continuous wearable-device collection; source data still must be ALCOA+: ① **non-team-member collection**: home nurses use validated eSource/ePRO devices + user authentication (attributable), time-stamps (contemporaneous), electronic signature / role log; the sponsor must train + qualify + quality-agreement + audit the external organization, with a traceable data chain (home-nurse collection → centralized EDC/eSource → cleaning) (data chain see §4.1). ② **massive raw wearable data**: not all need to be traditional source data — distinguish the continuous raw stream (stored as a machine-readable raw dataset, with device ID / firmware / algorithm-version metadata) from **analysis-relevant source** (endpoints derived per pre-specified SAP, e.g. average heart rate within the visit window); ALCOA+ applies to derived endpoints + raw traceability; recommend exporting full raw volume to controlled storage (with retention policy), using pre-defined derived values for analysis. ③ **QC system**: device calibration, data-transmission validation, missing-data handling rules, centralized-monitoring KRI for wearable-data integrity, discrepancy management. Regulatory expectations for DCT, wearables as source data and retention period are dynamic items, subject to official source text (verify officially).

### 4.1.3 Biospecimen chain of custody & shipping temperature deviation (workflow E / G)

The **integrity (chain of custody)** of biospecimens (e.g. PK blood samples) must run through the whole collection → processing → storage → transport → testing process: ① a transport temperature deviation (e.g. 4 hours exceeding 2–8°C en route) **does not mean the result is directly usable** — temperature exceedance may degrade analytes (especially proteins / nucleic acids / unstable metabolites), destroying sample representativeness; ② correct process: immediately record the deviation (start/end, peak, duration, device, finder), **isolate that batch's results and mark "pending evaluation"**, launch a deviation investigation (stability data, central-lab opinion, whether the sample can be re-collected); ③ the **sponsor / central-lab quality responsible person (not the investigator alone)** decides whether the result is usable, or whether re-collection / supplementary collection is needed; ④ if the investigator directly enters it into the EDC because "the result looks normal", the CRA must query and require completion of the evaluation and audit trail, not silently accept; ⑤ the specimen chain throughout (collection time, processing, temperature log, handover sheet) must be ALCOA+ traceable, retained as source data. Sample-transport acceptance criteria and disposition authority are dynamic items, subject to protocol / IB / central-lab SOP and GCP (verify officially).

#### 4.1.4 Subject withdrawal / revocation documentation & ICF archiving (workflow E / D) — post-enrollment subject documentation & data handling after consent withdrawal
- **Correct handling path**: ① the subject may withdraw informed consent at any time unconditionally (§2.2 ongoing consent); withdrawal is not retroactive to use of already-collected data (per protocol / IB); ② complete a **Withdrawal of Consent** form or have the investigator record the withdrawal statement (date, method, witness, reason for refusal to continue), archived together with the original ICF; ③ assess whether **post-withdrawal safety follow-up** is still needed (e.g. long half-life, known delayed risk) — this follow-up usually still must be completed and documented; ④ analysis-set assignment: per pre-specified SAP rules (e.g. still in ITT / FAS, safety by actual exposure); withdrawal ≠ automatic removal (echoes §4.2.2); ⑤ electronic data: stop new data collection for the withdrawn subject, retain locked data; if personal-data deletion is requested, assess per data-privacy and contract (see `ref-reg-cn.md` §8.5); ⑥ original ICF, withdrawal documents, screening / enrollment records are archived as **essential documents** in ISF / eTMF, retained for the same period as the trial document obligation (echoes §4.1.1).
- ⚠️ Withdrawal-form template, post-withdrawal follow-up scope, data-deletion right and retention period are dynamic items, subject to current GCP, data-protection regulations and protocol / contract (verify officially).

#### 4.1.5 Biospecimen destruction & secondary-use authorization (workflow E / G) — residual samples are not freely disposable
- **Correct handling path**: ① destruction: per protocol / IB / central-lab SOP and contract, destruction must be recorded (sample ID, quantity, method, executor, date) with destruction evidence retained; ② secondary use: off-protocol / new-study use of residual samples requires **re-consent or specific authorization** (broad consent / dynamic consent) — must not exceed the original ICF scope; ③ if the original ICF included a "future research" broad authorization approved by the ethics committee, use per the agreement and leave an audit trail; otherwise a new application is required; ④ the sample chain (§4.1.3) and privacy (§8.5) must run through the entire destruction / reuse process; ⑤ the destruction or reuse decision must be auditable and included in ISF / eTMF.
- ⚠️ Sample retention / destruction period and broad-consent scope are dynamic items, subject to ethics norms, GCP and data-protection regulations (verify officially).

#### 4.1.6 Biospecimen long-term storage & stability (workflow E / G) — storage conditions & expiry of retained samples
- **Correct handling path**: ① **storage conditions**: per sample type (serum / plasma / DNA / tissue) specify temperature / freezing / anti-degradation, with equipment calibration and alarms (echoes §3.11 temperature control); ② **stability**: retained-sample stability data support usability within the storage period; expiry requires assessment; ③ **expiry & destruction**: retention period per protocol / regulation (echoes §4.1.5 destruction authorization); destroy on expiry with evidence; ④ **central-lab calibration**: testing equipment must be calibrated and QC-controlled for comparable results; ⑤ **chain & privacy**: ALCOA+ throughout storage (§4.1.3) and de-identification (§8.5).
- ⚠️ Sample storage conditions, stability validation and expiry requirements are dynamic items, subject to protocol / IB / central-lab SOP and GCP (verify officially).

### 4.2 CRF, database lock & e-systems

The CRF collects only data supporting endpoints / safety / protocol execution / interpretation, with explicit units / timing / options / missing-reason / logic, consistent with protocol, SoA, data standards, SAP. Before lock confirm key-data completeness, acceptable queries, external data readiness, coding & reconciliation done, important deviations classified, analysis-set decisions recorded; unlock records reason / impact / approval / correction / relock. EDC / ePRO / IRT are selected, validated, configured, tested, go-live, interfaced, run, changed, retired per intended use & risk; least-necessary permissions; key changes assess data-integrity / blinding / analysis impact. Randomization (stratification / block / allocation concealment / drug coding / permissions) and unblinding (only for genuine medical need, record reason / time / role / impact) jointly protect the allocation process.

### 4.2.1 Randomization system & emergency supply (workflow E)

When the central randomization (IWRS / IRT) fails and a subject urgently needs drug, a pre-specified SOP may activate emergency manual allocation: use sealed backup codes / pharmacy backup drug, maintaining blinding (who may unblind, when, why — leave an audit trail), and record into the system immediately after allocation with reconciliation. Emergency allocation must pre-define trigger conditions, authorization, blinding protection, post-hoc reconciliation and audit trail in the SOP / randomization manual; afterwards assess impact on randomization and blinding and whether reporting is needed. IWRS permissions, configuration and emergency process must be validated and trained (system selection / validation see §4.2).

### 4.2.2 Emergency unblinding (unmasking assignment for rescue) (workflow E)

Emergency unblinding differs from the "emergency supply" in §4.2.1: it is **unmasking the treatment arm the subject belongs to** for the subject's rescue need. Authorization: the investigator applies per pre-specified SOP / blinding-maintenance plan, executed by the central randomization (IRT) for emergency unblinding, recording reason, time, executor and impact on blinding. Post-unblinding data handling: the subject is still analyzed by **original randomization assignment (ITT principle, preserving randomization integrity)**; unblinding information is blinded to efficacy-blind assessors; safety analysis by actual treatment received. Analysis-set assignment for subjects who withdraw before dosing: ① **ITT / FAS usually includes the subject** — under the ITT principle, all randomized subjects allocated a drug number, even if not dosed / withdrawn, still belong to ITT / FAS (preserving randomization integrity, avoiding selective-exclusion bias); ② only under **pre-specified** exclusion rules (e.g. never exposed and a major protocol violation) may removal from FAS be considered, but it must be pre-defined and written clearly in the SAP; ③ the safety set is counted by actual exposure. Withdrawal ≠ automatic removal from ITT/FAS; assignment must be pre-specified, transparent and traceable (analysis-set definition see `ref-reg-stats.md` §3.2). The unblinding itself must leave a complete audit trail, with post-hoc assessment of whether reporting is needed / whether it affects other subjects' blinding. Who may unblind, the unblinding process and data handling are dynamic items, subject to protocol / SOP and current GCP (verify officially).

### 4.2.3 Investigational product temperature excursion & cold-chain deviation handling (workflow E)

Investigational product must be stored per protocol / IB / label requirements (commonly 2–8°C refrigeration); a temperature deviation (e.g. refrigerator failure exceeding range) **does not mean it can be normally dispensed**: even with no visible change, chemical properties / potency / stability may be affected, and the deviation itself breaks "drug accountability and quality traceability". Correct process: ① **immediately isolate** the out-of-spec batch, suspend dispensing, apply a "pending evaluation" label; ② record the whole deviation (start/end time, peak temperature, duration, device, environment, finder); ③ launch a **deviation investigation**: assess impact on drug quality and subject safety (stability data, retain sample, supplier opinion); ④ the **sponsor / pharmacy and quality responsible person (not the pharmacy administrator alone)** decides destruction or restricted use after stability justification, with a conclusion based on quality evidence and an audit trail; ⑤ if subjects have already used drug from the deviation period, assess safety impact, with medical monitoring and reporting as needed, and notify the ethics committee; ⑥ root cause (equipment failure / alarm failure / personnel negligence) and CAPA (alarm, backup refrigerator, continuous temperature monitoring, SOP training). The CRC / pharmacy administrator **has no authority to unilaterally decide normal dispensing** — "no visible change = no big deal" is a typical wrong judgment. Specific storage conditions, deviation acceptance criteria and disposition authority are dynamic items, subject to protocol / IB / GCP and the quality system (verify officially).

### 4.2.4 Investigational product accounting, return & count discrepancy (workflow E)

Investigational product must establish **drug accountability**: dispensing, use, return, destruction and on-hand quantity recorded per box / per bottle and traceable to the subject. When a **count discrepancy** appears (e.g. dispensed 120 tablets, returned + destroyed 110, difference 10): ① do not treat it as "subject probably discarded" and close it directly — launch a **discrepancy investigation**: reconcile dispensing records, subject medication diary / returned residual drug, destruction records, transfer records, inventory timing, to locate the discrepancy source (recording error / not returned / lost / stolen); ② the CRA must record the discrepancy, launch query / deviation assessment, with the **pharmacy administrator + sponsor drug-supply / quality responsible person** judging whether it constitutes a protocol deviation or a major drug-management problem; ③ if the discrepancy cannot be reasonably explained or is suspected of drug loss, escalate reporting, assess subject safety (misdose / overdose), with medical monitoring and reporting as needed, and notify ethics; ④ root cause and CAPA (strengthen return process, medication-diary reconciliation, periodic inventory, dual-sign transfer); ⑤ accountability and investigation records must be ALCOA+ audited and retained as essential documents. The CRC / pharmacy administrator **has no authority to unilaterally deem "no big deal" and skip investigation** — drug-accountability completeness is a core GCP requirement. Count-discrepancy investigation authority, deviation determination and retention requirements are dynamic items, subject to protocol / IB / GCP and the quality system (verify officially).

#### 4.2.5 Minimization randomization & allocation concealment (workflow E) — balanced allocation must strictly prevent information leakage
- **Correct handling path**: ① **minimization**: dynamically balances allocation based on enrolled subjects' prognostic factors; must be executed automatically by IRT without revealing the next patient's allocation tendency to the site, preventing manipulation; ② **allocation concealment**: the random sequence is generated and held by the central randomization system; the investigator receives the allocation only after enrollment, preventing prospective knowledge; ③ IRT configuration: stratification factors, blocks (if used), minimization weights, emergency unblinding (§4.2.2) permissions must be pre-specified and validated (§4.2 system validation); ④ unblinding risk: leakage of minimization / stratification information causes selection bias; audit the randomization log and access permissions; ⑤ consistent with SAP: the randomization method is clearly written in the SAP and randomization manual.
- ⚠️ Minimization implementation, randomization configuration and validation requirements are dynamic items, subject to protocol / SAP and current statistical norms (verify officially).

#### 4.2.6 Investigational product recall & systemic-defect retrieval (workflow E) — systemic quality defect found during the trial
- **Correct handling path**: ① **isolate & recall**: immediately isolate the relevant batch, suspend dispensing, launch the recall procedure, notify all site pharmacies to stop use; ② **investigate & decide**: the quality responsible person assesses the defect's impact on subject safety (already-used batches), with medical monitoring, ethics / regulatory reporting and subject notification as needed; ③ **systemic defect**: if systemic (affecting multiple batches / sites), expand the investigation, CAPA (supplier / process), and assess impact on overall trial data reliability; ④ record: recall scope, batch numbers, execution, returned quantity, subject impact and corrections, ALCOA+ audit trail; ⑤ connects with §4.2.3 cold-chain deviation and §4.2.4 accounting — recall is the escalation of defect handling.
- ⚠️ Recall procedure, reporting path and timelines are dynamic items, subject to GCP, the quality system and regulatory defect-reporting requirements (verify officially).

#### 4.2.7 Drug blinding & packaging (double-blind consistency / emergency envelope) (workflow E) — blinding maintenance starts at packaging
- **Correct handling path**: ① **blinding & packaging**: active drug and placebo are identical in appearance, packaging and labeling; an independent blinding party packages per the random sequence, with the blind code sealed and held; ② **emergency envelope**: each subject / each box has an emergency unblinding envelope, broken only for genuine medical need (echoes §4.2.2 emergency unblinding), with record and audit trail; ③ **blinding maintenance**: pharmacy / CRC must not access the blind code; dispense by random number; accidental unblinding (e.g. packaging mix-up) must be reported, impact on blinding assessed, and the subject's data handled per actual treatment if warranted; ④ the blinding scheme and blinding-check are written in the blinding-maintenance plan; ⑤ damaged / missing emergency envelopes must be reissued and recorded.
- ⚠️ Blinding / packaging specifications and emergency-envelope management requirements are dynamic items, subject to protocol / SOP and GCP (verify officially).

#### 4.2.8 Response-adaptive randomization (RAR) (workflow E / C) — dynamically adjusting allocation ratio by efficacy
- **Correct handling path**: ① **RAR logic**: based on enrolled subjects' response / endpoint, raise the allocation probability of the better arm, increasing subjects' expected benefit; ② **bias control**: pre-specify the adaptive rule and validate by simulation (type I error controlled), preventing selection bias from interim-data leakage; ③ **link to SAP**: allocation rule, adaptation time points and blinding maintenance clearly written; ④ **coordinate with §4.2.5 minimization / allocation concealment**: RAR still needs allocation concealment and randomization-log audit; ⑤ **regulatory communication**: seek pre-agreement at Pre-IND / Type B (echoes §3.5 Bayesian adaptive).
- ⚠️ RAR acceptability, simulation and bias-control requirements are dynamic items, subject to current FDA / EMA / NMPA adaptive-design guidance (verify officially).

### 4.3 Data query lifecycle (workflow G · execution layer)

Query loop: open → classify (logic check / entry discrepancy / manual query) → route to responsible person (investigator / site / lab) → respond & correct (at source, do not overwrite original values, keep audit trail) → verify → close or reopen. Query types: system auto-check (edit check / logic gate), manual spot-review, external-data comparison; metrics include open-query count, age, distribution by site / variable. Before closing confirm key-data completeness, important deviations classified, analysis-set decisions recorded (see §4.2 pre-lock checklist). Queries must not be silently ignored; important items need handling records — do not cover un-traceable corrections with "resolved". The data manager **has no authority to unilaterally modify EDC original values or force-close queries**: when the site replies "confirmed correct" but DM review still finds it unreasonable, re-send the query requiring source-document evidence / review rationale; if the site insists, escalate to medical monitor / investigator for decision, with on-site source-document comparison if needed; all handling audited. All corrections must be made at source (see §3.4 SDV discrepancy loop).

### 4.4 Missing-data pre-specification, CDISC coding & CSR transparency (workflow C / G)

First determine the missingness mechanism: **MCAR / MAR / MNAR**. Non-random missingness (e.g. concentrated in later-enrolled subjects) tends toward MNAR; multiple imputation (MI, assuming MAR) used alone will systematically bias the conclusion — this is a real risk point for regulatory acceptability. **The SAP must be finalized before lock and before unblinding**: primary analysis (clearly defining analysis sets and missing-data handling, e.g. mixed model / MI under MAR) + **sensitivity analysis (required, for MNAR)**: reference-based imputation (e.g. J2R/CR) or tipping-point analysis, proving conclusion robustness; primary/sensitivity distinct, multiplicity controlled (ref `ref-reg-stats.md` §3.3). **CDISC compliance**: SDTM (data presentation) + ADaM (analysis dataset) must code missingness — ADaM requires pre-defined derivations, missing reasons marked with dedicated variables (e.g. `DTYPE`, missing-reason domain) so "why missing" is traceable. **CSR transparency (ICH E3)**: present primary analysis + all sensitivity analyses, missingness-pattern plot (by enrollment time), statement of MAR-assumption violation, not hiding MI assumptions. Regulatory acceptability: MI is acceptable under MAR but must sit alongside MNAR sensitivity analysis; the "MI + reference-based imputation + tipping-point" combination satisfies both statistical rigor and regulatory acceptability. Regional preferences for missing-data methods and CDISC implementation requirements are dynamic items, subject to official guidance / source text (verify officially).

# Data Systems, Coding & QC (workflow G/E · 4.5-4.16)


### 4.5 Statistical programming & TFL QC (workflow G / C) — ADaM definition, reproducibility & third-party programming handover
- **Correct handling path**: ① **ADaM definition**: ADaM datasets must carry pre-specified SAP-derived variables (analysis variables, missing flags `DTYPE`, intercurrent-event handling), traceable to SDTM mapping (missing-data coding echoes §4.4); ② **reproducibility**: versioned programming scripts, fixed random seed, documented runtime environment — same input rerun yields same output; ③ **TFL programming QC**: independent double programming (or programming + independent QC comparison), output consistent with SAP / shell spec, unified decimals / units / definitions; ④ **consistency with SAP**: TFL shells reviewed by statistics and medicine; primary / sensitivity / subgroup presentation definitions consistent with SAP; ⑤ **vendor handover**: define spec, acceptance criteria, deliverables, audit trail and knowledge transfer for outsourced programming, avoiding "black-box delivery"; ⑥ programming outputs as CSR supporting evidence, included in eTMF.
- ⚠️ ADaM / SDTM implementation-guide versions, TFL shell spec and QC requirements are dynamic items, subject to current CDISC standards and regulatory implementation requirements (verify officially).

### 4.6 Computerized system validation (CSV) & pre-go-live EDC validation (workflow G / E) — data integrity from the source
- **Correct handling path**: ① **CSV principle**: EDC / IRT / ePRO and similar systems must be validated per intended use and risk (IQ / OQ / PQ or equivalent), with configuration, testing and release documented (echoes §4.2 system selection / validation); ② **build & UAT**: after CRF modeling, perform user acceptance testing covering edit checks (logic / range / required), skips, derivations, exports; ③ **edit-check governance**: edit-check rules pre-specified in SAP / data-management plan, changes under version control, avoiding "over-editing" that blocks valid entry or "under-editing" that misses errors; ④ sign **system go-live** approval before launch, with issues traceable to test evidence; ⑤ vendor systems need vendor assessment and ongoing ops / SOP alignment.
- ⚠️ CSV guidance (e.g. GAMP5), EDC validation and audit-trail requirements are dynamic items, subject to current regulatory computerized-system-validation guidance (verify officially).

### 4.7 Data governance & data-quality framework (workflow G / H) — quality-responsibility chain from source to CSR
- **Correct handling path**: ① **governance scope**: data standards (CDISC SDTM / ADaM), metadata, coding (dictionary version), data flow and interfaces, quality metrics; ② **data-quality dimensions**: completeness / accuracy / consistency / timeliness / traceability (ALCOA+, §4.1); ③ **roles & responsibility**: the sponsor bears ultimate responsibility for data integrity (§2.3); DM, statistical programming, IT and vendors have clear division; ④ **process control**: the DMP specifies collection, cleaning, coding, transfer, retention and archiving rules, with changes under version control; ⑤ **link to technical base**: aligns with §4.6 CSV — system validation is the technical base of data governance; ⑥ **quality metrics**: use KRIs (§3.6) to monitor data-quality trends, triggering investigation on anomalies.
- ⚠️ Data-governance framework, CDISC standard versions and data-quality standard requirements are dynamic items, subject to current CDISC standards and regulatory data-governance guidance (verify officially).

### 4.8 Concomitant medication collection & assessment (workflow E / G) — concomitant medication recording & causal stratification
- **Correct handling path**: ① **collection**: at each visit systematically collect drug name, indication, start/stop, dose, timing vs study drug (echoes SDV §3.4); ② **coding**: standardize per pre-specified dictionary (e.g. MedDRA / WHODrug), fixed version (echoes §4.7 data governance); ③ **stratified assessment**: distinguish "allowed / prohibited / to-be-recorded" concomitant medication (per protocol); prohibited drugs constitute a protocol deviation (§6.2); ④ **endpoint impact**: concomitant medication can be analyzed as confounder / concomitant drug (linking §1.3 comparator, DDI §1.5), with pre-specified analysis handling; ⑤ **safety**: concomitant medication is a covariate for SUSAR aggregation and AE causal assessment (§4.3 / §4.5).
- ⚠️ Concomitant-medication collection scope, coding-dictionary version and prohibited-drug assessment are dynamic items, subject to protocol / SOP and current data standards (verify officially).

### 4.9 Blind data review (BDR) & pre-lock cleanup (workflow G / C) — final data-quality gate before unblinding
- **Correct handling path**: ① **BDR timing**: before DBL, before unblinding, review key quality issues in blind state (missing, logic, deviation, external-data readiness); ② **review content**: data completeness (key fields), residual edit checks, external data (central lab / imaging / third party) alignment, coding consistency, deviation classification; ③ **link to SAP**: confirm analysis-set decisions, missing-data handling and intercurrent-event strategy are pre-specified and ready (§4.4); ④ **actions**: open queries / deviations found at BDR must be closed or have reasons recorded before unlock; ⑤ **blinding maintenance**: BDR keeps the treatment-arm blind throughout; participants sign confidentiality / blinding commitments; ⑥ **lock**: DBL needs approval, version lock, unlock audit trail (§4.2).
- ⚠️ BDR process, pre-lock checklist and blinding-maintenance requirements are dynamic items, subject to current GCP and CDISC data standards (verify officially).

### 4.10 Clinical-trial system cybersecurity & data protection (workflow G / E) — EDC / IRT / ePRO system security
- **Correct handling path**: ① **security baseline**: access control, encrypted transmission / storage, audit logs, vulnerability management and penetration testing, layered on §4.6 CSV validation; ② **data minimization**: collect only necessary data, de-identify / pseudonymize (echoes §8.5); ③ **third party / cloud**: sign DPA (§8.5) with EDC / CRO / cloud, with sub-processor security management; ④ **incident response**: data breach must be reported per GDPR / local law (§8.5); ⑤ **link to GCP**: ALCOA+ traceability (§4.1) and system security jointly safeguard data integrity.
- ⚠️ System security baseline, encryption and breach-reporting requirements are dynamic items, subject to current regulatory cybersecurity guidance and data-protection laws (verify officially).

### 4.12 Electronic data migration & system decommissioning (workflow G / E) — system retirement without losing traceability
- **Correct handling path**: ① **migration**: pre-defined mapping, validation (field / coding consistency), parallel comparison and rollback plan; ② **decommissioning**: export data in a controlled format (e.g. long-term readable archive), with metadata and dictionary version attached; ③ **traceability**: ALCOA+ throughout migration / decommissioning (§4.1), audit-trail retained; ④ **link**: with §4.1.1 ISF / eTMF and §4.6 CSV — validation and archiving documented; ⑤ **retention**: post-retirement retention must meet regulation and contract (§4.1.1).
- ⚠️ Data-migration validation, archive format and retention requirements are dynamic items, subject to current regulatory data-management and CSV guidance (verify officially).

### 4.13 Central imaging & independent image assessment (workflow G / E) — consistency assurance for imaging endpoints
- **Correct handling path**: ① **independent review**: set up BICR / independent central imaging (IRC), blinded, with pre-specified charter (echoes `ref-reg-submission.md` §5.14 BICR charter); ② **acquisition standard**: unified series / protocol / QC, transmission and archiving (§4.1); ③ **bias control**: consistency with investigator assessment (Kappa), blinded and independent; ④ **data chain**: imaging data under ALCOA+ (§4.1), version traceable (QC §4.5); ⑤ **endpoint**: imaging endpoints need pre-specified assessment criteria (RECIST v1.1 etc.).
- ⚠️ Imaging-assessment charter, acquisition standard and assessment criteria (RECIST etc.) are dynamic items, subject to current RECIST / regulatory imaging-endpoint guidance (verify officially).

### 4.14 Scale / patient-reported outcome (PRO) translation & linguistic validation (workflow G) — conceptual equivalence not literal translation
- **Correct handling path**: ① **process**: forward translation - back translation - expert review - cognitive interview - finalization; ② **conceptual equivalence**: not literal translation; cultural / semantic equivalence first; ③ **electronic deployment**: ePRO (§4.10) version management and multilingual sync; ④ **link to COA (§5.24)**: scales are clinical outcome assessment tools; validation supports endpoint acceptability; ⑤ **record**: each language version and validation report archived.
- ⚠️ Scale-translation validation method and COA acceptability are dynamic items, subject to current FDA PRO guidance / ISPOR linguistic-validation norms (verify officially).

### 4.15 Laboratory normal ranges (LLN / ULN) & clinical-significance assessment (workflow G / E) — abnormal value ≠ adverse event
- **Correct handling path**: ① **range source**: use central-lab or protocol-specified normal ranges, distinguishing LLN / ULN; ② **grading**: abnormal (high / low) graded by magnitude and clinical significance (CS / NCS); ③ **consistency**: multicenter needs unified assessment rules, consistent AE coding (§4.7 MedDRA); ④ **link to AE**: whether an abnormal value counts as AE / SAE (§5.4) depends on clinical significance and study assessment; ⑤ **link to safety**: aggregate common abnormal signals (§4.8).
- ⚠️ Normal-range, abnormality grading and clinical-significance assessment are dynamic items, subject to current lab and AE-assessment norms (verify officially).

### 4.16 Investigational-product coding & blinded supply (workflow E) — physical guarantee of allocation concealment
- **Correct handling path**: ① **coding**: drug coding must be blinded per random number (linking §4.2.1 randomization), label de-identifies treatment arm; ② **packaging**: in double-blind double-dummy, placebo / active identical in appearance; ③ **supply**: IRT dispenses by random number; investigator cannot know the group; ④ **emergency unblinding**: when genuinely needed for rescue, per §4.2.2 emergency unblinding; ⑤ **link to accounting**: consistent with coded-drug accountability (§4.2.4 / §3.23).
- ⚠️ Drug-coding, blinded-packaging and supply requirements are dynamic items, subject to GCP and blinding-design specifications (verify officially).
