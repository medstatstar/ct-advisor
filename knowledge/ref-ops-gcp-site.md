# GCP & Role Responsibilities (workflow D · execution layer)


## 2. GCP & role responsibilities (workflow D · execution layer)

> The **current GCP version & implementation status** follow the GCP version discipline section of `ref-reg-contract.md`; this section covers execution principles only.

### 2.1 Ethical judgment order

Scientific necessity → risk reasonable & minimized → benefit–risk acceptable → fair subject selection → genuine informed consent → adequate privacy & data protection → ongoing oversight. Scientific validity is itself an ethical requirement: a study that cannot answer its question forces subjects to bear burden with no benefit.

### 2.2 Informed consent is a continuous process

Includes appropriate information, understandable expression, sufficient time, autonomous decision, opportunity to ask, no undue influence, correct version, process record, ongoing update. When new risks / procedure changes / information that may affect willingness to continue arise, assess whether to update consent and re-consent; special populations need extra assessment of consent capacity, legal representative, the subject's own wishes, minimal risk and direct benefit.

### 2.3 Responsibilities may be delegated, accountability does not disappear

Investigator / institution (on-site medical decisions, eligibility, consent, protocol execution, source data, investigational product, reporting, safety); sponsor (design, resources, quality system, safety oversight, vendor supervision, cross-site risk, regulatory communication, evidence integrity); CRO / vendor (leave verifiable evidence per contract & quality agreement); monitor (verify protection, protocol, key data, issue closure); IRB / IEC (independently review benefit–risk, consent, recruitment, changes, safety); QA (independent of evaluated activity, identify systemic risk and escalate).

#### 2.4 PI delegation to sub-investigator (sub-I) & supervision (workflow D) — delegation does not transfer oversight responsibility
- **Scenario / role**: investigator (PI) — in a multicenter trial the PI delegates part of the duties to a sub-I; how to handle a sub-I exceeding authorization (e.g. independently judging eligibility / dispensing study drug).
- **Correct handling path**: ① the PI is the GCP accountability entity; all delegated duties must be written into the **delegation log** with clear effective date and scope (echoes §3.1.2); ② the sub-I acts only within authorized scope; operations beyond authorization (e.g. screening / dosing without authorization) must be recorded as a **protocol deviation / qualification issue**, tracing the root cause (unclear authorization / insufficient training); ③ the PI's supervision responsibility for the sub-I does not transfer — periodically review sub-I operations, re-train, and withdraw authorization when necessary; ④ major over-authorization (affecting subject safety / data reliability) escalates to QA / ethics.
- **Key points**: the delegation log is auditable evidence of the responsibility boundary; the PI's oversight responsibility does not disappear by delegation (echoes §2.3).
- ⚠️ Authorization scope, sub-I qualification and supervision requirements are dynamic items, subject to IRB SOP and current GCP (verify officially).

#### 2.5 Subject complaints & rights-appeal channel (workflow D) — accessible, independent, audited (incl. subject appeal)
- **Scenario / role**: subject rights — a subject believes they were improperly enrolled / improperly treated; how to complain, independently appeal and obtain compensation.
- **Correct handling path**: ① the site must publicize the **complaint channel** (contact / ethics / independent appeal route); the ICF informs the subject of rights and appeal method; ② complaints must be recorded, investigated, with feedback timeline and responsible person; major complaints (harm / rights violation) escalate to ethics and sponsor; ③ compensation: handle subject-damage compensation per contract / insurance / local regulation (distinct from §3.10 participation compensation — compensation is for harm, not participation); ④ appeal independence: an appeal handler independent of investigator / sponsor may be set up (ethics or third party); ⑤ remediation: systemic issues revealed by complaints go through CAPA (§6).
- **Key points**: the right to complain is core to subject rights; there must be an accessible, independent, audited channel; compensation differs in nature from participation compensation.
- ⚠️ Complaint-handling timeline, compensation standard and insurance requirements are dynamic items, subject to local regulation, IRB SOP and GCP (verify officially).

#### 2.6 Investigator financial conflict of interest (COI) disclosure & management (workflow D) — equity / consultancy fees must be transparent
- **Scenario / role**: investigator (PI) — the PI holds equity or receives consultancy fees from the study-drug company; how to disclose, manage and decide on participation.
- **Correct handling path**: ① the investigator must **disclose before trial start** all relevant financial interests (equity, advisory, patent, speaker fee), written into the IRB submission and institutional COI policy; ② the institution / ethics assesses the conflict level: a major conflict must be **managed, reduced, or excluded** from participation (e.g. that investigator does not enroll / assess); ③ participation decisions must be independent of financial interest; subject informed consent need not disclose details but the institution must keep an audit trail; ④ links to §3.1.2 PI change: a new PI also needs a COI statement; ⑤ ongoing disclosure: new interests during the trial must be supplemented.
- **Key points**: COI core is "disclosure + management", not blanket exclusion; undisclosed major financial interest is a compliance defect.
- ⚠️ COI disclosure threshold and managing-institution policy requirements are dynamic items, subject to institutional SOP, IRB and current GCP (verify officially).

#### 2.7 Electronic informed consent (eConsent) & remote acquisition compliance (workflow D) — electronic signature & process audit trail
- **Scenario / role**: investigator / CRC — using electronic informed consent (eConsent, incl. remote video explanation / electronic signature) to obtain consent must meet legal and ethical requirements equivalent to paper.
- **Correct handling path**: ① **equivalence principle**: eConsent must carry all elements of the paper ICF (information, understanding, voluntariness, sufficient time, questions, withdrawal right), with an auditable acquisition process; ② **electronic signature**: meet reliable electronic-record / electronic-signature requirements (e.g. in the spirit of 21 CFR Part 11 or local equivalent), with identity verification, time-stamp, immutability, audit trail; ③ **remote explanation**: video / remote-meeting explanation must leave an audit trail, ensuring the investigator (or authorized person) is present and the subject fully understands; ④ **version control**: eICF version switch / re-sign same as paper (echoes §3.5.1 / §3.17); ⑤ **withdrawal**: electronic withdrawal must be recordable and archived (echoes §4.1.4).
- **Key points**: eConsent core is "auditable process + reliable signature", not simply putting a PDF online; remote acquisition must not weaken voluntariness and understanding.
- ⚠️ Electronic-signature / electronic-record regulation, remote-consent and identity-verification requirements are dynamic items, subject to FDA 21 CFR Part 11, NMPA electronic-medical-record / GCP and current local data law (verify officially).

#### 2.8 Waiver & emergency-context informed consent (workflow D) — waiver / emergency consent boundary
- **Scenario / role**: ethics / investigator — emergency rescue (e.g. stroke / trauma) or minimal-risk research proposes to apply for waiver / deferred consent; where is the boundary.
- **Correct handling path**: ① **waiver premise**: only when risk does not exceed minimal risk and consent cannot be obtained in advance (e.g. retrospective chart study, anonymous samples) may the IRB approve a waiver or simplified consent; ② **emergency context**: the subject cannot express wishes and no legal representative is promptly available (e.g. coma emergency), per local law / IRB approval deferred or surrogate consent may be used, with post-hoc prompt notification and obtaining consent (if feasible); ③ **no generalization**: therapeutic intervention must not evade consent by "emergency"; ④ **document**: waiver / emergency consent needs IRB written approval and reason recorded; the ICF must still cover accessible information; ⑤ **link**: with §2.2 ongoing consent and §2.7 eConsent.
- **Key points**: waiver / emergency consent is a strictly limited exception — needs IRB approval, minimal risk, post-hoc notification; must not be generalized into the norm.
- ⚠️ Waiver / emergency-consent applicability, approval procedure and post-hoc notification requirements are dynamic items, subject to IRB SOP and current GCP / ethics norms (verify officially).

#### 2.10 Subject privacy & data-protection implementation (privacy by design) (workflow D / G) — engineering privacy into the trial
- **Scenario / role**: investigator / DPO — subjects' personal health data in the trial must embed privacy protection from the design stage, not a post-hoc patch.
- **Correct handling path**: ① **data minimization**: collect only necessary data, de-identify / pseudonymize first (echoes §8.5); ② **access control**: role-based least privilege, audit trail (echoes §4.6 CSV / §4.10 cybersecurity); ③ **consent link**: privacy purpose and cross-border disclosure written into ICF (§2.2 / §8.13); ④ **retention & deletion**: retention period and deletion right pre-specified (§4.1.4); ⑤ **cross-border**: cross-border transfer needs compliance assessment (§8.5 / §8.13).
- **Key points**: privacy protection must be "embedded by design" — minimization + pseudonymization + access control + deletion right form an auditable privacy loop.
- ⚠️ Privacy-protection requirements, de-identification and deletion right are dynamic items, subject to GDPR / PIPL and current data-protection law (verify officially).

#### 2.11 Investigator-initiated trial (IIT) governance (workflow D / registration) — responsibility boundary of non-sponsor-led trials
- **Scenario / role**: investigator / institution office — an IIT initiated by an investigator (hospital / academic); how to govern the sponsor role and responsibility, funding and conflict of interest.
- **Correct handling path**: ① **accountability entity**: in an IIT the investigator / institution often acts as sponsor and must bear GCP sponsor responsibility (§2.3) — design, quality, safety, reporting; ② **funding / collaboration**: industry-funded IIT must be transparent and not interfere with scientific independence and data interpretation (echoes §8.7 MSL boundary); ③ **ethics & regulation**: IIT also needs ethics review, CTA / filing, SAE / SUSAR reporting; ④ **distinction from industry-sponsored trials**: responsibility, insurance (§3.16), TMF requirements are consistent but the entity differs; ⑤ **quality**: IIT also needs a risk plan (§3.14) and monitoring / audit (§3.4 / §6.4).
- **Key points**: IIT does not change the nature of GCP responsibility — when the investigator / institution acts as sponsor it must fully assume sponsor obligations; the funder must not erode scientific independence.
- ⚠️ IIT sponsor responsibility, funding disclosure and regulatory requirements are dynamic items, subject to IRB SOP and current GCP (verify officially).

### 2.12 Women of childbearing potential (WOCBP) pregnancy screening & contraception management (workflow D / F) — pregnancy-exposure prevention triad
- **Scenario / role**: investigator / medicine — trials of drugs with reproductive risk must implement pregnancy screening and effective contraception for women of childbearing potential (WOCBP) to prevent pregnancy exposure.
- **Correct handling path**: ① **definition & exclusion**: define WOCBP and exclusion evidence (menopause / sterilization); ② **screening**: before enrollment and periodically (e.g. per cycle / per month) high-sensitivity pregnancy test (serum / urine hCG); positive → exclude / withdraw; ③ **contraception**: effective contraception during the trial and washout period (per risk sheet / dual contraception), written into ICF (§2.1); ④ **failure handling**: pregnancy exposure reported and followed up as SAE / special process (§5.4), with a pregnancy-outcome registry provided; ⑤ **male partner**: a male subject's teratogenic risk also needs contraception / barrier; ⑥ **linkage**: with §5.6 reproductive safety, §5.7 pregnant women, §5.8 lactation.
- **Key points**: WOCBP management is the triad "screening + contraception + failure handling" — prevention of exposure precedes after-the-fact remediation.
- ⚠️ WOCBP definition, pregnancy-test timing / sensitivity and contraception requirements are dynamic items, subject to ICH M3(R2) / current reproductive and GCP guidance (verify officially).

# Site Activation & Monitoring (workflow E · 3.1-3.13)


## 3. Clinical operations (workflow E)

### 3.1 Site activation quality gate

Feasibility confirmed → responsibilities / contract / budget clear → ethics & regulatory conditions met → team delegation & training complete → systems / supplies / drug / lab ready → written release. A system flag "activated" ≠ the site is safe to screen & enroll.

### 3.1.1 Institution filing prerequisite (workflow E)

To conduct a drug clinical trial in China, the institution must have completed filing in the NMPA drug clinical trial institution filing system (filing system, not certification); an unfiled institution may not start screening / enrollment. The pre-start chain at the site: confirm institution filing status and filed specialty / PI qualification → contract and budget → ethics and filing conditions → team delegation and training complete → systems / supplies / drug / lab ready → written release (see §3.1). Filing status is per the NMPA filing system public record (the match between specific specialty / PI qualification and the trial is a dynamic item requiring official verification); a proposed new filed specialty must go through the institution filing-change process — no "start first, file later". **Institution filing and ethics-committee approval are two independent prerequisites**: in China, starting screening / enrollment requires simultaneously ① the institution filed in the NMPA filing system (with filed specialty / PI matching the trial) ② ethics-committee review approval ③ contract in effect, etc. A site that has only completed filing but not passed ethics review **must not begin screening or enrollment** — filing status ≠ ethics approval; both are required to start (pre-start chain see §3.1).

### 3.1.2 Principal investigator (PI) change procedure & document update (workflow E / D)

When the PI withdraws due to retirement / resignation / qualification change, **the new PI's succession must complete the formal change procedure — no "take over first, paperwork later"**: ① the hospital issues a PI-change letter confirming the new PI's professional qualification, GCP certificate and match with this trial (echoes §3.1.1 filed specialty / PI qualification); ② update the **delegation log** — add the new PI, remove the original PI, with clear duty division and effective date; ③ archive the new PI's CV / GCP certificate / conflict-of-interest statement; ④ re-train (protocol / SOP / safety / data system) and keep records; ⑤ **update the ethics committee and institution filing in sync**: PI change usually requires ethics review (major change per institution requirement), and the PI information in the filing system must be changed accordingly; ⑥ update the PI accountability entity in contract / budget / insurance; ⑦ inform subjects (e.g. for ongoing-treatment / follow-up researcher change). Mere "same qualification" of the investigator does not justify skipping the procedure — the PI is the GCP accountability entity, and the change must be transparent, auditable and known to all parties. The specific change-reporting scope and ethics / filing requirements are dynamic items, subject to institutional SOP and current GCP (verify officially).

### 3.2 Site feasibility & CRO supervision

Site feasibility verifies patient source, screening funnel, competing trials, key exams, rescue capability, team capacity, staff stability, prior quality, emergency care, data entry, drug / sample conditions, ethics & contract cycle, travel & follow-up burden; recruitment forecast states base / conversion / ramp / seasonality / site differences and calibrates against actual data. Vendor supervision chain: capability & compliance due diligence → scope & RACI → quality agreement → interface & escalation → deliverables & KPI → subcontractor change control → performance review. High-risk interfaces (safety database / central lab / imaging / IRT / ePRO / drug supply / external transfer) define data owner, frequency, format, version, verification, anomaly & escalation.

### 3.3 Risk-based monitoring & site closeout

Identify key data & key processes first, then configure centralized / remote / on-site activities; fixed visits or 100% SDV cannot replace risk judgment. Before closeout confirm safety follow-up, data queries, drug reconciliation, samples, finance, reporting & archiving are all closed; open items keep owner, deadline and tracking.

### 3.4 SDV & source data verification (workflow E)

SDV verifies CRF entry against source documents (medical record / lab / prescription / diary) — it is "monitoring" not "copying"; risk-based monitoring first defines key data / key processes, then the SDV scope (full / sampling / targeted), fixed 100% SDV or fixed visits cannot replace risk judgment (see §3.3). Discrepancy loop: find → classify (entry error / protocol deviation / data-integrity issue) → trace to source document and responsible person → correct at source (do not overwrite original value, keep audit trail) → re-verify → close; entry errors go through query (see §4.3), protocol deviations go through §6 deviation process, suspected data-integrity issues escalate to QA. SDV findings must leave auditable evidence (which source, which item, who, when, how changed). **Baseline values near the exclusion / inclusion boundary + subsequent safety event**: values not exceeding the threshold are not currently an eligibility deviation, but must not be silently passed — record the context and let the medical monitor judge whether it signals the subject was inherently high-risk; if the investigator insists on the original judgment while the CRA has reasonable doubt, raise a query / escalate to medical monitor or QA, and apply enhanced monitoring for similar boundary values at that site (echoes §3.3 risk-based).

### 3.4.1 Monitoring visit report (MVR) timeline & quality (workflow E)

After a monitoring visit (on-site / centralized), a written monitoring report must be completed promptly to solidify findings, corrective actions and the tracking loop. Timeline: **the specific completion and submission days for the monitoring report are set by the sponsor SOP / monitoring plan** (commonly within 10–15 working days after the visit; the specifics are dynamic items requiring official verification); overdue must record the reason and escalate. The report must include: visit type / date / scope, SDV proportion and findings, protocol deviations, safety-report review, drug accountability, subject recruitment and retention, corrective actions (owner / deadline), open-item tracking, and overall site-quality assessment. The MVR is auditable evidence of the GCP "monitoring" obligation (echoes §3.4 discrepancy audit trail) and must not be replaced merely by in-system logs; the report timeline and minimum-content requirements are dynamic items, subject to sponsor SOP and current GCP (verify officially).

### 3.5 Protocol amendment classification & ethics review (workflow D)

Amendments are divided into substantive (affecting subject safety / rights, scientific validity, endpoint, inclusion-exclusion, dose, design) and administrative (contact info, dates, formatting, no effect on risk–benefit). Substantive amendments (e.g. inclusion criterion age upper limit 65→75, primary-endpoint change) need convened IRB review; administrative may use expedited review; before review: change reason and approval, impact on enrolled / not-yet-enrolled subjects, version switch, training, ICF / CRF / EDC / IRT / SAP / supplies sync (also see §8.1 cross-file dependencies). Amendment classification and review procedure are dynamic items, subject to IRB SOP and current GCP (version see `ref-reg-gcp-version.md` §6).
- **Amendment example (lab change)**: changing the central lab to a local lab, even if the testing methodology and reference ranges are unchanged, because the sample flow / storage / testing entity change may affect data reliability and safety-monitoring consistency, is generally judged a **substantive amendment** requiring convened IRB review; if a written quality assessment proves true equivalence with no risk–benefit impact, it may be argued as administrative (expedited). Regardless of classification, assess the comparability impact on already-collected data and update related SOPs / manuals / supplies accordingly.

### 3.5.1 Informed-consent execution quality & acquisition qualification (workflow D)

Informed consent must be obtained by a qualified person: the investigator, or a person formally authorized by the investigator, trained and designated per SOP (e.g. CRC / research nurse) may participate in explanation and assist completion after the subject has sufficient time to understand and ask questions; but **the investigator bears the primary responsibility for "voluntary, fully-informed" consent**; the consent process should be completed with the investigator present, and both signatures executed at the same process node — **no retroactive signing**. A CRC explaining and the investigator "coming back to sign later" reveals the investigator's absence at consent, a process defect — should: ① pause the process until the investigator returns, or have another authorized person execute per rules; ② if retroactive signing already occurred, record it as a process deviation, trace the root cause (unclear SOP authorization boundary), and correct by training; ③ the SOP must clarify "who may obtain consent, when the investigator must be present, the authorization boundary". The consent version, language comprehension and withdrawal-right notification are dynamic items, subject to current GCP and ethics norms (verify officially).

**ICF version update & re-signing**: When an ICF version is updated during the trial due to new safety information / procedure change, enrolled and on-treatment subjects must re-obtain informed consent — ① assess whether the change affects willingness to continue (§2.2 ongoing-consent principle): new information such as non-serious but common adverse reactions that "affects risk perception" usually requires **re-signing the new ICF version**; ② those refusing re-signing: must not be forced to continue; handle per protocol / SOP (may withdraw or only complete safety follow-up), and record the refusal reason and communication; ③ version switch must sync update of authorization, training, supplies; the old ICF and signing records are archived and traceable. The re-signing scope and refusal handling are dynamic items, subject to IRB SOP and current GCP (verify officially).

**Pediatric subject Assent & guardian conflict**: For children with corresponding maturity (e.g. 8–12 years), obtain their **Assent (child-version consent / assent)**, with the guardian signing informed consent; Assent and guardian consent are two independent documents. When a child clearly refuses to continue (e.g. unwilling to give blood again) but the guardian insists, **the child's refusal takes precedence over the guardian's request** — the study must follow the child's best interest and minimal-harm principle, and must not force the child to endure procedural pain "because the guardian thinks it is good"; should: ① pause the invasive procedure, assess the child's willingness and maturity, communicate with the family; ② if subject safety / key treatment is involved, launch investigator and ethics assessment; ③ if necessary, submit to the ethics committee for emergency review, adjusting the subject's participation scope or withdrawal. The pediatric Assent age, refusal handling and ethics-review requirements are dynamic items, subject to 《儿科人群药物临床试验技术指导原则》 and current GCP (verify officially).

### 3.6 Unified KRI system & regionally differentiated monitoring plan (workflow E)

E6(R3) is the current GCP; risk-based monitoring (RBM) is embedded as a principle baseline, not an add-on; fixed visits or 100% SDV cannot replace risk judgment (see §3.3). For international multicenter trials we recommend: first define **critical quality factors (CtQ) → map to key risk indicators (KRI)**; common KRI dimensions include enrollment pace / eligibility rate, data quality (entry discrepancy rate, query rate, window violation), protocol compliance (deviation rate / important deviations), safety-reporting timeliness, blinding maintenance, investigational-product accountability; use **centralized monitoring as the backbone** (remote trend analysis and visualization, finding issues before on-site). **Unified framework, differentiated thresholds**: the global master monitoring plan defines a unified KRI taxonomy and risk-grading logic; regional appendices differentiate SDV sampling ratio, tolerance limits, cross-border data and monitoring-report requirements — satisfying both FDA's RBM expectation and, when NMPA focuses on SDV ratio, substituting "targeted SDV after risk justification" for full coverage. During the E6(R2)→E6(R3) transition, the monitoring plan must reflect the RBM principle baseline (GCP version judgment see `ref-reg-gcp-version.md` §6). Regional cross-border data, current SDV-ratio expectations per country, and the E6(R3) implementation transition date are dynamic items, subject to current official FDA / EMA / NMPA·CDE text (verify officially).

### 3.7 Adaptive FIH trial ongoing ethics supervision (workflow D / B)

An adaptive first-in-human (FIH) trial has an internal safety monitoring committee (iDMC) dynamically deciding dose / cohort based on PK/PD; its risk comes from the "decision process" not a fixed protocol, so the ethics committee's initial review must review the **decision framework** not just the fixed protocol: ① the iDMC **charter** must be submitted to the IRB — including member independence, operating rules, unblinding procedure, and reporting obligation to the IRB; ② **pre-specified adaptive decision rules** (dose escalation/expansion, pause, termination criteria) must be transparent; the IRB reviews the rules themselves; ③ **ongoing ethics supervision mechanism**: pre-set IRB re-review triggers — when an iDMC decision changes subsequent subjects' risk/benefit (dose escalation, cohort expansion, safety pause) it automatically triggers IRB re-review; iDMC meeting summaries (blinded or appropriately unblinded) are periodically submitted to the IRB as continuing-review material; ④ **adaptive-change pre-classification**: within-framework changes (expedited) vs those needing IRB re-approval (substantive), defined at the outset; ⑤ **independence firewall**: iDMC independent from the sponsor operations team; the IRB must understand the isolation structure to assess conflict-of-interest control (FIH design side see §1.5). The IRB continuing-review frequency, iDMC charter GCP requirements and adaptive-trial ethics-review details are dynamic items, subject to current GCP and ethics-review norms (verify officially).

**Continuing review (track review) frequency**: The ethics committee must periodically track-review an approved trial; the maximum interval usually **does not exceed 12 months** (specifically per current GCP / ethics-review norms, a dynamic item requiring official verification); when the continuing review is overdue, **enrolled subjects usually may continue per-protocol drug / follow-up** (treatment should not be interrupted merely due to IRB administrative delay), but the sponsor / investigator must proactively urge the IRB to complete review and keep an audit trail; major safety signals still require immediate ethics reporting. The maximum continuing-review interval and overdue handling are dynamic items, subject to current GCP and ethics-review norms (verify officially).

### 3.8 Single-arm trial external-control pre-specification & conditional-approval path (workflow B / C / registration)

The regulatory acceptability of a single-arm design depends on the indication context: **where there is no standard therapy (e.g. rare disease with no SOC) or RCT is ethically / feasibly impossible, single-arm (especially as the pivotal trial for conditional approval) is the scenario CDE is relatively most receptive to**; conversely, indications with an existing SOC (e.g. most solid tumors) single-arm is usually viewed as insufficient evidence level and needs strong justification. Given external-control bias risk, the protocol must **pre-specify (not post-hoc)** (echoing the 5-point pre-specification in this section): ① comparable-population definition (tumor type / line / molecular feature / baseline) pre-aligned with the single-arm cohort; ② matching / adjustment method (PS, MAIC) and covariates locked; ③ time-trend control (contemporaneity of historical cohort, treatment background); ④ measurement consistency (RECIST version, whether BICR); ⑤ a pre-specified "external-control limitations" section in the protocol (a CDE review must-see). **Estimand (E9 R1)**: the intercurrent-event (new treatment start, death before progression, loss to follow-up) strategy for ORR/DoR pre-defined; **sensitivity analysis** must be primary + at least 1–2 (different intercurrent-event strategies, different external-control calibers, stress test), with primary/sensitivity distinct and multiplicity controlled (multiplicity see `ref-reg-stats.md` §3.3). **Endpoint matrix**: ORR + DoR + PFS + OS, avoiding single-point ORR as sole evidence. Conditional-approval logic: single-arm pivotal trial + commitment to post-marketing confirmatory trial (often RCT or high-quality external / real-world control); single-arm is staged evidence not final evidence (evidence standard, MCID, biomarker subgroup, label balance see `ref-reg-submission.md` §5.4). **Pre-IND (Type B) communication is strongly advised and better early**: the acceptability of single-arm + external control + conditional approval highly depends on CDE pre-agreement; topics include single-arm acceptability premise, external-control construction method, ORR+support matrix, confirmatory-commitment structure, whether to pursue breakthrough therapy. The conditional-approval applicability standard, single-arm pivotal-trial acceptance boundary, confirmatory-trial timeline and communication-meeting procedure are dynamic items, subject to current CDE official text (verify officially).

**Specific methods for RWE external control (echoes §3.8)**: Building external control from RWD must meet the core requirements of CDE《真实世界证据支持药物研发与审评的指导原则》 — ① **RWD applicability**: the data source (registry / insurance / EMR) must match the research question; data quality (completeness / accuracy / timeliness) assessed as acceptable; ② **causal-inference rigor**: pre-specify the study design (retrospective cohort / nested / external-control arm), target-trial emulation, comparable-population definition; ③ **bias control**: selection bias (use PS / MAIC / entropy-balancing to match baseline covariates), confounding bias (sensitivity analysis / negative-control / positive-control test), time trend (contemporaneous control); ④ **transparent reporting**: pre-specified limitations section and bias-direction stress test. RWE acceptability highly depends on the pre-registered protocol and prior regulatory agreement (echoes Pre-IND, §3.8). The specific RWE applicability standard and causal-inference method requirements are dynamic items, subject to current CDE official text (verify officially).

**Alternative to placebo-controlled design (rare disease / high ethical-pressure scenarios)**: When placebo control draws strong public / patient-organization opposition (e.g. rapidly progressing fatal rare disease ALS), consider alternative designs — ① **Delayed-Start design (randomized withdrawal)**: all subjects first receive active treatment; a random subset delays start for a period, indirectly demonstrating efficacy by "benefit difference between early vs delayed start", balancing ethics; its statistical assumptions (e.g. comparability of early-start and delayed groups in the common observation window, washout effect) must be pre-defined and regulator-accepted; ② **external control (see §3.8)**: use historical / RWE control instead of concurrent placebo; ③ **adaptive / Bayesian design** to reduce placebo exposure. Alternative-design evidence level is usually weaker than RCT placebo control; pre-specify the statistical assumptions in the protocol and communicate with regulators early (Pre-IND / Type B). The regulatory acceptability and statistical-assumption requirements of delayed-start and similar designs are dynamic items, subject to current GCP / statistical norms and CDE text (verify officially).

### 3.9 Cell / gene therapy long-term follow-up (LTFU) compliance (workflow E / registration)

Cell / gene therapy products such as CAR-T require long-term follow-up (commonly up to 15 years) due to delayed adverse reactions (secondary malignancies, long-term immune / organ toxicity); the regulatory obligation (e.g. gene-therapy-product long-term-follow-up guidance) and practice (relocation / loss to follow-up / subjective refusal) must be balanced: ① **independent LTFU plan and informed consent**: LTFU may be separated from the original treatment-trial consent, with a dedicated long-term-follow-up consent and compensation mechanism to reduce subject burden and dropout; ② **executable follow-up path**: combine registry, referral network, subject self-report, insurance / prescription-record linkage; graded tracking for loss to follow-up (phone / letter / third party); ③ **regulatory obligation not waived by loss to follow-up**: the sponsor must demonstrate reasonable efforts were made and document the loss-to-follow-up reason; cannot terminate the follow-up obligation merely because contact is lost; ④ **data quality**: LTFU data (adverse events / secondary cancer) must be ALCOA+ traceable; ⑤ subjects who withdraw consent stop new data collection but already-collected data are retained per agreement. The long-term-follow-up duration, minimum follow-up content and dropout handling are dynamic items, subject to current gene-therapy / cell-therapy long-term-follow-up guidance (verify officially).

### 3.10 Subject compensation & inducement-bias prevention (workflow E / D) — approval, recording and ethics boundary for transport / nutrition compensation
- **Scenario / role**: investigator / CRC / sponsor — a subject applies for compensation for follow-up transport and lost wages; clarify the approval chain, payment record and whether it constitutes undue inducement.
- **Correct handling path**: ① compensation must be **reasonable, based on actual occurrence, and not tied to completing the study** (avoid "fixed amount per visit" per-visit payment that induces blind retention); ② the standard is written into protocol / IB / ICF and subject handbook; the ICF transparently discloses compensation items and amounts; ③ approval and payment: investigator / CRC verify actual occurrence (fare, lost-wage proof) → sponsor finance pays per SOP and keeps an audit trail (avoid unrecorded cash); ④ from the subject-rights angle: compensation must not affect the voluntary withdrawal right, and must not force continuation due to non-payment; ⑤ ethics review: the compensation plan (amount, method, recipient) must be submitted to ethics review; excessively high compensation may be viewed as undue inducement; ⑥ record: payment ledger, tax handling per local regulation.
- **Key points**: compensation "reimbursement + reasonable lost wage" is acceptable; "payment rising with completion" easily constitutes inducement; ICF transparent disclosure + ethics review + finance audit trail are the three controls.
- ⚠️ Compensation amount cap, tax and cash-payment limits, ethics-review requirements are dynamic items, subject to local regulation, IRB SOP and current GCP (verify officially).

### 3.11 Site warehouse / logistics & transport temperature-control failure handling (workflow E) — temperature logger failure during sponsor-warehouse shipment
- **Scenario / role**: site warehouse / logistics / CRA — investigational product shipped from the sponsor warehouse to the site; the temperature logger fails en route with no data; whether it can be used on arrival and how to handle.
- **Correct handling path**: ① on arrival **isolate** that batch, apply a "pending evaluation" label, suspend dispensing; ② launch a deviation investigation: record transport start/end, duration, environment, logger-failure evidence, packaging integrity, arrival appearance; ③ assess: use **retain-sample stability data + supplier quality opinion + packaging validation (e.g. insulated-box validated duration)** to infer whether the drug may have exceeded conditions during transport; ④ the **sponsor quality / drug-supply responsible person** (not logistics or pharmacy alone) decides usable / restricted / destroy; ⑤ if already dispensed to the site and used, assess subject safety, with medical monitoring and reporting as needed, and notify ethics; ⑥ root cause (logger calibration / redundancy, carrier SOP) and CAPA (dual logger, real-time temperature alarm, arrival temperature recheck).
- **Key points**: temperature-logger failure ≠ "usable if no over-temperature evidence" — missing data itself breaks traceability; the decision authority is the quality responsible person; warehouse / logistics have no unilateral release authority.
- ⚠️ Transport acceptance criteria, logger-redundancy requirement and stability-inference rules are dynamic items, subject to protocol / IB / GCP and the quality system (verify officially).

### 3.12 Institution-entity change (merger / split) filing & document transfer (workflow E / D) — qualification, contract and drug accountability succession for the ongoing trial
- **Scenario / role**: institution office / sponsor — the hospital to which the site belongs merges / splits, changing the legal entity; how to transfer and report the NMPA institution filing, contract, ethics and drug accountability of the ongoing trial.
- **Correct handling path**: ① the new legal entity must confirm it still holds NMPA drug clinical-trial institution filing qualification (filed specialty / PI matching the trial, echoes §3.1.1); entity change usually requires a **filing change / re-filing** process — no "continue first, file later"; ② contract and budget: the original contract-entity change requires a **tripartite supplementary agreement** or re-signing, with the insured entity updated accordingly; ③ ethics: report the institution-entity change to the ethics committee, reviewing the new entity's qualification and subject-protection continuity; ④ essential documents (ISF / eTMF) and investigational-product accountability: **wholly transferred** to the new entity per contract / quality agreement with a transfer list (echoes §4.1.1); ⑤ inform subjects and link informed consent (researcher / institution change may affect ongoing treatment); ⑥ if the PI moves with the institution, follow §3.1.2 PI change.
- **Key points**: institution merger / split does not automatically "inherit" the ongoing-trial qualification — filing entity, contract and ethics must be changed in sync and audited; drug accountability and documents must be auditable on transfer.
- ⚠️ Filing-change procedure and entity-qualification succession requirements are dynamic items, subject to the NMPA filing system and current GCP (verify officially).

### 3.13 CRO quality audit & transition management (workflow E) — sponsor ultimate responsibility not transferred
- **Scenario / role**: sponsor / CRO — the CRO fails to execute the monitoring plan or quality is substandard; how to trigger audit, CAPA and smooth transition.
- **Correct handling path**: ① per §3.2 vendor supervision chain: KPI / deliverable deviation triggers a **vendor audit (quality audit)** (scope, notice, on-site, finding grading); ② audit findings must have a **corrective and preventive action (CAPA)** plan, responsible person and timeline, tracked to closure by the sponsor; ③ transition: CRO change / termination needs a **transition plan** (knowledge transfer, in-progress data / document transfer, subject continuity, regulatory / ethics reporting), avoiding "gap"; ④ contract and quality agreement clarify audit right, termination conditions, data ownership and transfer standard; ⑤ major quality failure (e.g. systemic SDV absence) must assess impact on already-produced data reliability and report.
- **Key points**: CRO errors are borne by the sponsor as ultimate responsibility (§2.3) — audit and transition are extensions of the sponsor's supervision obligation.
- ⚠️ CRO audit frequency and transition-reporting requirements are dynamic items, subject to contract / quality agreement and current GCP (verify officially).
