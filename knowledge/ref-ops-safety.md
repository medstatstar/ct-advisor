# Safety Execution — Individual Case Layer (workflow F · execution)


## 5. Safety execution (workflow F · execution layer)

> The safety **regulatory chain** (SUSAR / RSI / DSUR regulations, RSI rules, oncology SUSAR aggregation, DSUR package) is in `ref-reg-safety.md` §4; this section covers individual-case handling.

### 5.1 Separate six judgments first Within the protocol's collection scope → meets seriousness criteria → mild / moderate / severe → causal relation to investigational intervention → meets applicable RSI expectation → which recording / notification / expedited-report / cumulative / periodic path. Seriousness ≠ severity; causality ≠ whether to record; expectation & report path also depend on RSI version, jurisdiction, role, awareness date.

### 5.2 Individual-case handling loop Immediate medical treatment → obtain key facts → classify & medical assessment → report per applicable path → ongoing follow-up → assess impact on other subjects & the study overall → update documents / communication / risk control (follow-up information such as discharge summaries and diagnostic certificates must be used to update the initial SAE through a follow-up report / amendment, not filed as a separate standalone report). Medical assessment checks temporal relation, alternative causes, concomitant treatment, de-/re-challenge, outcome, follow-up completeness. Overdue is judged by event nature, first-awareness date, jurisdiction, ethics, protocol, SOP; never backdate or rewrite the timeline; when overdue, record the delay truthfully, assess subject-protection impact, find root cause, take CAPA.

### 5.3 Medical monitoring & post-marketing The medical monitoring plan defines data source / frequency, risk triggers, eligibility questions, dose adjustment, hold rules, emergency escalation, PV interface, blinding boundary, decision records. Post-marketing puts spontaneous reports / studies / literature / regulatory information into one signal workflow: detect / verify / prioritize / assess / decide / risk-minimize / communicate / effectiveness-evaluate; report count cannot stand for incidence, nor can missing data imply "no risk found".

### 5.4 Individual safety report clock & materials (workflow F · execution layer) The reporting path has two legs: investigator → sponsor (individual SAE reporting; timeline and format per protocol / SOP, commonly within 24 hours of awareness — the exact figure is a dynamic item and must be officially verified); sponsor → regulators and ethics (SUSAR expedited reporting; timeline and path in `ref-reg-safety.md` §4.5). The investigator's report materials contain at minimum: subject identification, the event, seriousness determination, onset / stop dates, causality assessment, expectedness (against RSI / IB), management and outcome (outcome information must be obtained through follow-up — when discharge summaries, diagnostic certificates and similar documents become available later, they should update the initial SAE via a follow-up report, consistent with GCP 2020 Article 26(2): "a detailed written follow-up report shall subsequently be provided in a timely manner"). Report on time but never fabricate a backdated timeline; when overdue, record the delay truthfully, assess the impact on subject protection, investigate the root cause and take CAPA. Reporting responsibility / recipients / forms follow 《药物临床试验期间安全性数据快速报告标准和程序》 (dynamic item, must be officially verified).

**Pregnancy as a safety event**: a pregnancy confirmed during the trial (even when the investigator judges it unrelated to the drug and the subject is in good condition) usually **must be reported to the sponsor as an SAE or safety event per protocol / SOP** (pregnancy itself is an important reproductive-safety signal, and drug-exposure risk must be assessed). Upon receipt the sponsor must: ① record and report it (path as above); ② provide pregnancy registration / contraception and risk counselling; ③ track the pregnancy outcome (miscarriage / live birth / congenital anomaly) and, where necessary, follow up through the drug pregnancy registry; ④ assess whether the subject's continued participation and dosing are affected; ⑤ where the drug carries reproductive / developmental risk, feed the pregnancy event into the RSI / IB safety information update. Whether pregnancy is reported as an SAE, and the registration and follow-up requirements, are dynamic items governed by the protocol / SOP and the expedited safety-data reporting standard (verify officially).

### 5.6 Integrated reproductive-toxicity evaluation & management of WOCBP / pregnant subjects (workflow F) — the cross-section of reproductive safety signals
- **Scenario / role**: PV / Medical — the investigational product has potential reproductive / developmental toxicity, so nonclinical reproductive toxicity, pregnancy events and risk assessment must be evaluated together.
- **Correct handling path**: ① **Nonclinical**: results of reproductive toxicity studies (fertility / embryo-fetal / peri- and postnatal) feed into the IB / RSI; ② **Clinical contraception**: apply the vehicle / drug contraception requirements in §5.8, managing contraception in women of childbearing potential and during the post-treatment washout period; ③ **Pregnancy events**: report, register and follow up per §5.4 and assess reproductive risk; ④ **Exposure–effect**: assess the association between reproductive risk and exposure level; ⑤ **Communication**: balanced disclosure of reproductive risk in the IB / ICF (§5.4, §5.5).
- **Key takeaway**: reproductive safety is an integrated evaluation of "nonclinical + clinical pregnancy events + contraception management", not a single-point signal.
- ⚠️ Reproductive-toxicity study requirements, contraception duration and pregnancy monitoring are dynamic items governed by ICH S5 and current reproductive-safety guidance (verify officially).

### 5.7 Special considerations for enrolling pregnant subjects (workflow F / D) — the inclusion boundary for the pregnant population
- **Scenario / role**: Investigator / Medical — the trial intends to enrol pregnant women (e.g., a pregnancy-related indication, or extrapolation that must cover women of childbearing potential), requiring special protection and design.
- **Correct handling path**: ① **Precondition for inclusion**: enrol only when scientifically necessary and the risk is controllable, avoiding a blanket exclusion that leaves an evidence gap; ② **Risk–benefit**: strengthened benefit–risk assessment (§5.6 reproductive safety) and independent ethics review; ③ **Monitoring**: pregnancy-outcome tracking (§5.4), fetal / neonatal monitoring, exposure registry; ④ **Separation from non-pregnant subjects**: analyse pregnant-subject data separately to avoid confusion with the general childbearing-age population; ⑤ **Linkage**: connect to consent in §2.2 / §3.5.1 and reproductive-system risk in §5.8.
- **Key takeaway**: enrolling pregnant women requires "necessity + strengthened protection + independent review" — filling the evidence gap while preventing harm.
- ⚠️ Inclusion conditions, monitoring and ethics-review requirements for pregnant subjects are dynamic items governed by current GCP and pregnancy drug-trial guidance (verify officially).

### 5.8 Lactating subjects & breastfeeding safety considerations (workflow F / D) — quantifying milk exposure and deciding on infant risk
- **Scenario / role**: Investigator / Medical — the investigational product may be excreted in breast milk or the subject is lactating, so breastfeeding exposure risk and exclusion / discontinuation decisions must be assessed.
- **Correct handling path**: ① **Exclusion / deferral**: lactating women are usually excluded or required to stop breastfeeding (depending on milk excretion and infant risk); ② **Risk assessment**: milk-to-plasma ratio (M/P), infant exposure level, half-life; ③ **Exposure handling**: accidental breastfeeding exposure must be reported and followed up (§5.4), with infant monitoring; ④ **Coordination**: coordinate with contraception / WOCBP (§2.12) reproductive risk management; ⑤ **Labelling**: affects the "use in lactating women" section of the label.
- **Key takeaway**: breastfeeding risk is a "milk-exposure quantification + infant risk" decision — exclusion first, with follow-up whenever exposure occurs.
- ⚠️ Lactation exclusion, breastfeeding-exposure assessment and labelling requirements are dynamic items governed by current GCP and pregnancy / lactation medication guidance (verify officially).

### 5.9 Subject death & the death reporting process (workflow F) — reportable regardless of causality
- **Scenario / role**: Investigator / PV — a subject death (whether or not drug-related) must be reported within the required timeline and analysed for attribution.
- **Correct handling path**: ① **Reporting**: death as an SAE (§5.4) must be reported to the sponsor / IRB / regulators within the prescribed timeline (commonly an initial report within 24 h); ② **Causality**: determine the relationship of the cause of death to the investigational product / underlying disease (§5.2 individual-case handling); ③ **Autopsy**: per protocol and family consent, clarify the cause of death as far as possible; ④ **Aggregation**: death events roll up into the DSUR / CSR; ⑤ **Follow-up**: a death occurring after withdrawal must still be ascertained through follow-up (§3.27).
- **Key takeaway**: death reporting is "on-time reporting + cause attribution + pursuing autopsy" — report regardless of causality.
- ⚠️ Death-reporting timelines, autopsy and attribution requirements are dynamic items governed by GCP and the current SAE-reporting standards (verify officially).

### 5.10 Overdose & accidental exposure handling (workflow F) — emergency care + reporting + follow-up
- **Scenario / role**: Medical / Investigator — a subject overdose or accidental exposure (e.g., accidental ingestion by a child, needlestick injury to a healthcare worker) must be managed and followed up per procedure.
- **Correct handling path**: ① **Management**: emergency care based on toxicity / mechanism, decontamination, symptomatic treatment and an antidote where necessary; ② **Reporting**: overdose / accidental exposure is usually reported as an SAE (§5.4), with particular attention to childbearing potential (§2.12) and lactation (§5.8); ③ **Follow-up**: clinical monitoring until recovery, with the outcome documented; ④ **Link to adherence (§3.25) / drug accountability count (§3.23)**: overdose reflects adherence or drug-availability problems; ⑤ **Link to the RMP (§4.9)**: overdose risk is taken into risk management.
- **Key takeaway**: overdose / accidental exposure is "emergency care + reporting + follow-up" — interlocked with adherence and risk management.
- ⚠️ Overdose / accidental-exposure management and reporting requirements are dynamic items governed by GCP and the current SAE-reporting standards (verify officially).

### 5.5 Adverse events of special interest (AESI) and IB / ICF safety wording (workflow F / D) **AESI (adverse event of special interest)** are events the sponsor proactively pre-specifies on the basis of "clinical importance + potential association + class-effect signal"; they **do not depend on an individual investigator's causality assessment** — an investigator judging an event "probably unrelated" is a common blinded single-case assessment bias, and the sponsor must perform an independent blinded re-assessment rather than using that judgement to exclude an AESI. Criteria for inclusion: a seriousness signal (e.g., events of the same type, including fatal ones, occurring in a small sample), class-effect plausibility, mechanistic plausibility → even when individual-case causality is undetermined, the event should be listed and enhanced monitoring, expedited reporting, dedicated IB / ICF language and investigator training should be activated. **IB description (in the spirit of CIOMS VI / E2A)**: state objectively the events observed (including fatal cases), make clear that "the causal relationship has not been established / an association with the investigational product cannot be fully excluded", and list risk-minimisation and monitoring requirements; the IB must reflect current safety knowledge. **ICF description (balancing ethical disclosure against drop-out risk)**: disclose serious risks honestly (including fatality) but use neutral causal wording ("potential association / not yet established / under investigation") and avoid the over-certainty of "definitely caused by the drug" — this satisfies ethics (major risks must be disclosed) without provoking unnecessary drop-out. The current versions of CIOMS VI and E2A, and the specific requirements for presenting safety information in the IB / ICF in each region, are dynamic items governed by the official source text (verify officially).

# QA, QC & CAPA (workflow D)


## 6. QA, QC & CAPA (workflow D)

QC is embedded in daily confirmation that specific steps / deliverables are correct; QA independently evaluates system effectiveness; sponsor supervision ensures delegated activities stay controlled. Deviations first assess impact on subject safety / rights, primary endpoint, randomization & blinding, data reliability, regulatory obligation, then classify & escalate; record fact / scope / time / immediate correction / root cause / CAPA / effectiveness / impact on analysis & report. Risk–quality chain: key data process → failure mode → risk → preventive & detective control → metric trigger → investigation → CAPA → effectiveness check → lessons learned. Risk is weighted by impact on subject protection & result reliability, not merely frequency. Audit evidence answers: what is required, what actually happened, how big the impact, why it happened, how to prevent recurrence. "More training" is usually a measure, not a root cause; seek root cause in process design / responsibility / workload / system / interface / knowledge / supervision / incentive.

### 6.2 Protocol-deviation examples & ethics reporting (workflow D) A deviation is first assessed for impact: subject safety / rights, primary endpoint, randomization and blinding, data reliability, compliance obligations; then classified (minor / important / major). Common examples: eligibility violations (e.g., enrolling an over-age subject or one who does not meet the inclusion criteria), visit-window excursions, dose / timing deviations, missed safety assessments, use of the wrong informed-consent version. Important / major deviations are reported to the ethics committee promptly per the protocol / IRB SOP (full-board or expedited review depending on the nature of the deviation and institutional requirements); the report package contains the fact, scope, timing, immediate correction, root cause, CAPA and impact on analysis and reporting; minor deviations may be reported in a periodic summary. Classification and reporting cadence are dynamic items governed by the protocol / IRB SOP (deviation-handling principles are also given in the §6 overview).

**Distinguishing PD from PV (under the current China GCP framework)**: ICH E6(R2) has folded "Protocol Violation" into the single category "Protocol Deviation", yet some domestic institutions still use "PV (protocol violation)" to designate major / substantive deviations. The practical distinction lies not in the wording but in the **degree of impact and the level of handling**: ① **PD (protocol deviation)** refers broadly to any departure from the protocol (minor, important or major); ② **PV (where an institution retains the term)** usually denotes specifically a **major / important deviation** (affecting subject safety, the primary endpoint, randomization and blinding, or data reliability), requiring escalation to QA, reporting to ethics and root-cause CAPA; ③ whether called PD or PV, classification must rest on "the impact on subject protection / result reliability" rather than on terminology — avoid the regulatory gap created by "only a PV gets reported, a PD does not". Institutional terminology conventions and reporting thresholds follow the IRB SOP and current GCP (dynamic item, must be officially verified).

### 6.3 Data-integrity fraud investigation and reporting (workflow D) — verify before characterising, act decisively once confirmed
- **Scenario / role**: Sponsor compliance / QM — suspected systematic CRF data fabrication at a site (identical entries / invented data) is discovered; how to investigate, report and protect data integrity.
- **Correct handling path**: ① Do not presume — **verify first** (source-document comparison, on-site SDV, interviews) and distinguish data-entry error and training problems from deliberate fabrication; ② if fabrication is confirmed: immediately **quarantine the affected data** and assess the impact on subject safety / the primary endpoint / the analysis; ③ report: depending on severity, notify the ethics committee, sponsor safety / compliance and, where necessary, the regulator (data fabrication is a serious compliance event); ④ correct: redo the affected data, retract / amend, launch a quality audit of the site and, where necessary, stop enrolment at that site; ⑤ root-cause analysis and systemic CAPA (tightened monitoring, source-data verification, personnel qualification); ⑥ document the entire process and protect the whistle-blower and the subjects.
- **Key takeaway**: a fraud investigation must "verify before characterising", but once confirmed the data must be quarantined and reported decisively — data integrity is a line that cannot be crossed.
- ⚠️ Fraud-reporting timelines and the regulatory reporting path are dynamic items governed by current GCP and regulatory compliance requirements (verify officially).

### 6.4 GCP inspection readiness and responding to findings (workflow D) — everyday traceability, and honesty in the response
- **Scenario / role**: Sponsor compliance / QA — facing a regulatory GCP inspection; how to prepare traceable materials and respond to inspection findings.
- **Correct handling path**: ① **Preparation**: ensure source data, ISF / eTMF, drug accountability, SAE / SUSAR and monitoring reports are traceable (ALCOA+, §4.1) and rehearse the traceability path; ② **On-site cooperation**: designate a liaison, provide requested documents promptly, and record truthfully what the inspector observes; ③ **Response to findings**: classify each finding (critical / major / observation) and give, item by item, **root cause + correction + CAPA + timeline + evidence**, concealing nothing; ④ major findings (affecting subject safety / data reliability) must be escalated and their impact on the submission assessed; ⑤ track remediation to closure with effectiveness verification, and archive the response and its evidence.
- **Key takeaway**: the core of inspection readiness is "traceability as a daily habit"; the value of a finding response lies in honesty and closure, not in argumentation.
- ⚠️ Inspection procedures, finding classification and response timelines are dynamic items governed by the current NMPA / FDA / EMA inspection standards (verify officially).

### 6.5 CAPA effectiveness check (workflow D) — proving the correction truly prevents recurrence
- **Scenario / role**: Quality management (QM) — the same type of deviation recurs after CAPA implementation; how to run an effectiveness check demonstrating that the corrective action works.
- **Correct handling path**: ① **Define success criteria**: set effectiveness metrics when the CAPA is opened (recurrence rate of the same event type, audit-finding closure rate); ② **Timing of verification**: set an observation window after implementation (e.g., 1–2 monitoring cycles / quarters) and use data to show the recurrence rate has fallen; ③ **Methods**: trend analysis (KRI, §3.6), repeat audit, source-data spot checks, training pass rates; ④ **If not passed**: return to root-cause analysis and redo it (§6: the root cause is not "more training") and escalate to management; ⑤ archive the effectiveness evidence in the CAPA record as audit evidence of quality-system maturity.
- **Key takeaway**: the closure point of a CAPA is "effectiveness verification", not "the action has been executed" — without verification it is not closed.
- ⚠️ CAPA effectiveness observation windows and metric requirements are dynamic items governed by the quality system and GCP (verify officially).

# Project Governance & CSR Execution (workflow G)


## 7. Project governance & CSR execution (workflow G)

### 7.1 Unified fact base (Study Profile) At minimum: objective, phase, design, population, intervention & comparator, endpoints, sample size, region, timeline, key risks, key document versions, important decisions, open items. Protocol / IB / ICF / SAP / safety docs / ops plan / TFL / CSR / registration materials all update from this same base.

### 7.2 Governance & CSR writing landing Define decision tier, RACI, meeting cadence, escalation threshold, decision log, change control; risk = uncertain event not yet occurred, issue = event already occurred needing handling, change = baseline adjustment after evaluated & approved. Before CSR writing, lock data cutoff, document versions, analysis sets, blinded-review decision, key outputs; the report explains why the study was designed this way, how it was actually executed, what deviations occurred, what the results can / cannot answer, how the benefit–risk is understood (regulatory structure in `ref-reg-submission.md` §5).
