# Safety Regulatory Chain — SUSAR/RSI/DSUR (workflow F)

> This file is the safety-chain document of the Regulatory & Statistical series (originally the §113–§156 area of ref-regulatory-statistical.md, merged and retained on 2026-08-05); cross-series basis is in ref-reg-contract.md; use `scripts/search_refs.py` for full-text location.

## 4. Safety regulatory chain (workflow F)

> Individual-case handling **execution** is in `ref-ops-safety.md` §5; this section covers the regulatory chain & cumulative evaluation.

### 4.1 SUSAR / RSI / DSUR loop `individual SAE → causality → expectation / RSI version → SUSAR expedited report → similar-event aggregation → signal → risk characterization → document & measure update → DSUR cumulative evaluation`.

### 4.2 RSI rules RSI sits in the IB and judges the expectedness of a suspected serious adverse reaction; it is not a full safety summary of the investigational drug, nor may its scope be widened to reduce SUSARs. RSI check: has the event already been observed with the investigational drug (mechanism / class speculation alone is insufficient); is there sufficient medical evidence for a reasonable causal relation; a single event is usually insufficient to include (unless strong evidence), repeat occurrence does not automatically mean include; SAEs whose relevance cannot be evaluated are handled with caution; fatal events of a not-yet-marketed investigational drug usually should not be written into RSI; an individual more severe / specific / frequent than the RSI description may still be unexpected; RSI uses MedDRA SOC / PT with an explainable denominator, not over-broad terms that hide risk; different indications / populations may be presented separately; when no expected serious adverse reaction yet exists, still keep an independent RSI section and explain. RSI change needs medical basis, change control, implementation date, impact assessment, version traceability, MedDRA impact; urgent safety information does not wait for the annual update — go through IB other-section update / protocol ICF amendment / urgent measure / regulatory communication.

### 4.3 Oncology SUSAR aggregation & signal action Cumulative analysis across at least six dimensions: count & incidence, severity & outcome, medical specificity, causality, similar-event aggregation (MedDRA / SMQ / predefined concept sets), concomitant medication. CDE oncology guidance suggests: early cumulative 2 cases (especially 3+) of the same PT / concept warrants high attention, and certain mechanism-related or severe individual cases trigger deep evaluation even at 1 case — **this is a signal-detection hint, not a universal statutory threshold**. After signal confirmation, actions: add visits / monitoring / pre-medication / dosing speed / stop-and-rechallenge rules; amend protocol / ICF / IB-RSI / monitoring / medical monitoring; re-train; assess enrollment / dosing / dose / termination hold; if it may significantly affect benefit–risk, follow paths like "other potential serious risk safety information" with regulatory communication; decision / owner / date / affected trials / effectiveness check feed the closed loop.

### 4.4 DSUR (ICH E2F) DSUR centers on the same active ingredient's global development safety information, linking reporting period, cumulative exposure, individual & cumulative, important findings, overall safety evaluation, important risks & conclusions. Core: `new info this period → compare with prior → does risk change → does benefit–risk change → actions taken / planned`. Before writing, lock DIBD / DLP / period / applicable RSI-IB version, global trial scope & responsibility, prior DSUR & regional addenda, safety database / SAR line listings / SAE cumulative / cumulative exposure, clinical / non-clinical / literature / post-marketing / regulatory measures, data cutoff & reconciliation status; for missing data make a gap table (owner / deadline / impact), never use "no obvious risk seen" to mask unreceived / uncoded / unfollowed / unreconciled data. Before submission check: no conflict in DIBD / DLP / period / version; global list consistent with exposure / line listing / cumulative scope; RSI change / signal / regulatory measure / risk control coherent; numbers / terms / MedDRA version / product name / important risks consistent; conclusion does not exceed evidence strength; regional deadline / addendum / entry verified against official original.

### 4.5 SUSAR expedited-reporting clock & pathway (workflow F) After receiving a SUSAR, the sponsor reports it on an expedited basis to regulators and ethics: fatal or life-threatening cases generally get an initial report within 7 days, other cases generally within 15 days (the exact timelines, recipients, pathways and forms follow 《药物临床试验期间安全性数据快速报告标准和程序》; dynamic items must be officially verified); a follow-up report is submitted after the initial report where necessary. SUSAR triggering is premised on the expectedness determination against the RSI (IB) (see §4.2), and the causality and expectedness assessments must be documented. SUSARs from overseas sites likewise enter the sponsor's global safety information flow and are reported in parallel per China requirements. The reporting clock starts from the "date of first awareness"; the timeline must never be fabricated retrospectively. When overdue, record the delay truthfully, assess the impact on subject protection, investigate the root cause and take CAPA (execution layer in `ref-ops-safety.md` §5.4). **Reporting recipients (three parties)**: the sponsor must submit the SUSAR expedited report to ① the Center for Drug Evaluation (CDE) of the NMPA; ② the provincial drug regulatory authority; ③ the ethics committee (the ethics committee of the site concerned and / or the central ethics committee, depending on the circumstances). SUSARs occurring at overseas sites likewise enter the global safety information flow and are reported in parallel to these three parties per China requirements. The precise scope of recipients, pathways and forms follows 《药物临床试验期间安全性数据快速报告标准和程序》 (dynamic item, must be officially verified).

### 4.6 Signal detection and disproportionality methods (PRR / ROR) (workflow F) — statistical tools for quantifying cumulative signals
- **Scenario / role**: PV / Biostatistics — use disproportionality analysis (PRR, ROR, EBGM, etc.) to quantify signals from spontaneous reports / cumulative SAEs, supporting SUSAR aggregation and signal determination (echoing the oncology SUSAR aggregation in §4.3).
- **Correct handling path**: ① **Methods**: disproportionality analysis (PRR proportional reporting ratio, ROR reporting odds ratio, IC information component, EBGM empirical Bayes geometric mean) quantifies the extent to which a given drug–event co-occurrence exceeds background; ② **Signal ≠ causality**: disproportionality only flags a signal; clinical / mechanistic assessment (causality, expectedness, seriousness) is required before it feeds into RSI / IB updates and regulatory communication (§4.2 / §4.4); ③ **Data sources**: spontaneous report databases, cumulative SUSARs and literature — computation requires data completeness and coding consistency (MedDRA); ④ **Threshold conventions**: the commonly used signal thresholds for each method (e.g., PRR ≥ 2 with χ² ≥ 4; ROR 95% CI excluding 1) are **reference conventions, not statutory standards**; ⑤ **Interaction with §4.3**: the "2-case / 3-case" hint in oncology SUSAR aggregation can be reinforced by disproportionality quantification.
- **Key takeaway**: disproportionality is a signal-screening tool, not causal evidence; thresholds are only references, and the closed loop ultimately rests on medical judgement plus regulatory communication.
- ⚠️ The choice of disproportionality method, signal-threshold conventions and regulatory acceptance are dynamic items governed by ICH E2E / CIOMS signal detection and the current PV guidance of each country (verify officially).

### 4.7 Individual case safety report (ICSR) handling chain and MedDRA coding (workflow F) — from the individual case to the database
- **Scenario / role**: PV — the end-to-end process for individual case safety reports (ICSRs), from collection, MedDRA coding and entry into the safety database through to consistency checking (echoing the individual-case clock in clinical §5.4).
- **Correct handling path**: ① **Collection and entry**: enter individual-case information in structured form, with causality / expectedness assessment (§4.2 RSI); ② **MedDRA coding**: code events in MedDRA (SOC / PT) — coding consistency drives aggregation and signals (§4.3); ③ **Safety database**: ICSRs enter the database and are integrated with SUSARs / periodic reports / literature; ④ **Consistency**: the database must agree with the CSR / SAE listings / line listings (§5.2 / §5.3); ⑤ **Timelines**: individual-case reporting timelines follow §4.5 / §5.4.
- **Key takeaway**: the ICSR is the atomic unit of safety information — coding consistency and database integration determine the reliability of all downstream aggregation and signals.
- ⚠️ ICSR handling timelines, MedDRA coding and safety-database requirements are dynamic items governed by 《药物临床试验期间安全性数据快速报告标准和程序》 and the current PV rules of each country (verify officially).

### 4.8 Safety database and the signal management workflow (workflow F) — from database to signal
- **Scenario / role**: PV / Biostatistics — maintain the safety database and perform signal detection, validation, prioritisation and risk assessment per §4.3 / §4.6 / §4.7.
- **Correct handling path**: ① **Database governance**: integrate ICSRs (§4.7), literature, spontaneous reports and study data, ensuring completeness and traceability; ② **Signal workflow**: detection (disproportionality §4.6) → validation (medical / mechanistic) → prioritisation (seriousness / class effect) → assessment → decision (RSI / IB update, regulatory communication §4.2 / §4.4) → risk minimisation → effectiveness evaluation; ③ **Signal thresholds**: method thresholds are reference conventions only (§4.6), with medical judgement decisive; ④ **Link to the safety plan**: a signal triggers an update of the safety plan and reinforcement of safety monitoring; ⑤ **Regulatory communication**: major signals go through the §4.4 DSUR route and urgent safety measures.
- **Key takeaway**: the database is the foundation of signal management — only the closed loop "detect → validate → prioritise → assess → decide → minimise" constitutes a system.
- ⚠️ Safety-database requirements, the signal management process and threshold conventions are dynamic items governed by ICH E2E / CIOMS and the current PV guidance of each country (verify officially).

### 4.9 Safety risk management plan (RMP / Safety Management Plan) (workflow F) — prospective management of safety risk
- **Scenario / role**: PV / Medical — develop a safety risk management plan (RMP / safety plan) to identify, minimise and communicate risk systematically (echoing signal management in §4.8).
- **Correct handling path**: ① **Plan content**: important identified risks, important potential risks, additional pharmacological / epidemiological data, risk-minimisation measures; ② **Link to RSI / IB**: risk updates are synchronised into the RSI (§4.2) and IB safety information; ③ **Link to signals**: a signal triggers a plan update and reinforced safety monitoring (§4.8); ④ **Link to regulators**: the RMP forms part of the submission / conditions of approval (particularly under conditional approval, §5.4); ⑤ **Effectiveness**: risk-minimisation measures must be evaluated for effectiveness (echoing CAPA effectiveness in §6.5).
- **Key takeaway**: an RMP turns "safety" from passive signal reception into active management — a closed loop of identify → minimise → communicate → evaluate effectiveness.
- ⚠️ RMP content, risk-minimisation measures and regulatory requirements are dynamic items governed by ICH E2E and the current RMP guidance of each country (verify officially).

### 4.10 Post-authorisation safety study (PASS) / post-marketing commitments (workflow F / registration) — completing the evidence after approval
- **Scenario / role**: PV / Regulatory — conduct a post-authorisation safety study (PASS) after approval, or fulfil post-marketing commitments (such as the confirmatory trial in §5.4), as required by the regulator.
- **Correct handling path**: ① **PASS types**: prospective / retrospective / registry-based evaluation of safety questions unresolved at the time of approval; ② **Link to conditional approval**: the confirmatory trial (§5.4) is the binding form of PASS and must be completed on time; ③ **Design**: pre-specified protocol and bias control (echoing RWE design in §3.11 and external controls in §3.8); ④ **Reporting**: results feed into the DSUR (§4.4) and periodic safety updates and affect the label; ⑤ **Regulatory**: non-fulfilment can trigger regulatory action.
- **Key takeaway**: PASS / post-marketing commitments are the "post-approval evidence loop" — under conditional approval in particular they are a hard obligation, requiring pre-specified design and on-time delivery.
- ⚠️ PASS design, timelines and fulfilment requirements are dynamic items governed by ICH E2E and the current post-marketing study guidance of each country (verify officially).
