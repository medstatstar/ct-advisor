# Statistics & Estimands (workflow C)


## 3. Statistics & estimands (workflow C)

### 3.1 Estimand chain (ICH E9(R1)) `trial objective → clinical question → estimand → main estimation method → sensitivity estimation method → estimate & interpretation`. An estimand defines at least: ① treatment condition; ② target population; ③ variable or endpoint; ④ intercurrent event and its strategy; ⑤ population-level summary quantity. Common intercurrent-event strategies: treatment-policy, hypothetical, composite-variable, while-on-treatment, principal-stratum; different events may use different strategies, each choice mapped to a clear clinical question. Distinguish: stopping treatment / taking rescue / crossover / death may be intercurrent events; dropping out or not collecting data creates missingness; sensitivity analysis addresses robustness of the main analysis' key assumptions under the *same* estimand; supplementary analysis answers a *different* question and cannot masquerade as sensitivity. The estimand must be written back into the protocol, data collection & follow-up must support it, the SAP must give a reproducible estimation method, and the CSR must interpret under the same clinical question.

### 3.2 Analysis set, missingness & minimal sample-size inputs
- Analysis set: Intention-To-Treat (ITT) / Full Analysis Set (FAS) preserves randomization benefit but "ITT" cannot replace a complete estimand; Per-Protocol (PP) supports compliance & non-inferiority robustness, post-hoc exclusion may bias; safety set organized by actual exposure, with pre-specified switch & multiple-exposure rules.
- Missingness: an intercurrent event changes *what* is estimated; missingness means data needed to estimate that target was not observed; first define the estimand and the data that should keep being collected, then discuss missingness mechanism, main estimation and sensitivity.
- **Minimal sample-size inputs**: main estimand and superiority / non-inferiority / equivalence / precision objective; main variable, analysis method and expected effect; standard deviation / event rate / risk ratio / correlation structure; one- or two-sided alpha, power, allocation ratio; multiplicity, interim analysis, clustering / repeated measures; dropout, unevaluable, compliance, event maturity. **Sample size is a function of assumptions, not a fixed truth; missing key parameters → give only the computation framework, parameter table and scenario analysis, no pseudo-precise number.**
- **Handoff to ct-samplesize**: once parameters are complete, route via workflow `C` to `ct-samplesize` for actual computation (see `scripts/workflows.json` `integration.sample_size_handoff`); this skill does not compute n in-house.

### 3.3 Non-inferiority, multiplicity & subgroups
- Non-inferiority margin must be argued from historical control effect and clinically acceptable loss; check constancy, assay sensitivity, compliance, crossover, rescue therapy, and the interpretation when FAS/ITT-class vs PP results disagree.
- Primary endpoint / dose / group / time point / interim decision together form multiplicity, requiring pre-specified error-rate control.
- Subgroups emphasize consistency & interaction; "one significant, one not" does not automatically prove a difference.

### 3.4 Interim analysis, futility boundary, DSMB & planned-deviation explanation (workflow C / D)
Prespecified interim analyses must distinguish **information time** (actual events / prespecified events) from calendar time: if prespecified by event count, the α-spending boundary is itself a function of the information fraction and running at 0.6 information fraction remains **within design**; but insufficient events reduce test power — the safer approach is to extend enrollment/follow-up to reach the target event count to preserve power, rather than merely running the interim early. **Futility analysis** crossing its boundary yields a valid but more uncertain conclusion; the key is not to change the boundary post hoc. **DSMB** may recommend continuation when event counts are insufficient or standard-of-care updates — this is a **planned deviation**, handled on the principle of **transparent recording rather than concealment**: record the original design assumptions (event count, α-spending function), the actual deviation (information fraction, SOC update), the DSMB's written risk/benefit re-assessment rationale, and submit the protocol amendment/deviation report to the regulator; explain to the regulator that "trial integrity is preserved" through **preset deviation handling + complete documentation** (may be pre-notified via Type B communication), stating how α is preserved and how the analysis plan is revised. The α-spending-function choice, interim-timing rules, and planned-deviation regulatory-reporting requirements are dynamic items — follow current GCP/statistical standards (officially verify).

**Post-hoc discovery of DSMB / iDMC member conflict of interest (COI)**: ① If a potential COI is discovered post hoc (e.g., serving on another sponsor ongoing-project SAB, reputational linkage), assess whether it materially affected the independence of completed interim analyses — a one-off, low-linkage (unpaid, no direct economic interest) relationship generally does not automatically invalidate completed data, but must be **documented and disclosed and independently re-reviewed**; ② Immediately supplement the COI declaration, remove the member from subsequent DSMB or set up a firewall (no participation in related voting), record the timeline; ③ Validity of completed interim-analysis data: if COI did not materially enter decision-making (independent statistical center, blinded data, written records prove it), it generally **remains valid**, but the relationship and corrective actions must be disclosed in the regulatory submission to ensure acceptability; ④ Material undisclosed COI that entered key decisions may require redoing or explanation. DSMB COI disclosure and data-validity determination requirements are dynamic items — follow ICH E6(R3) independent data-monitoring integrity requirements and current standards (officially verify).

### 3.5 Bayesian adaptive design: prior, stopping boundary & α equivalence (workflow C / B) — regulator justification of Bayesian interim stopping vs frequentist power
- **Scenario / Role**: Biostatistician — planning a Bayesian adaptive design (Bayesian interim efficacy/futility stopping, dose adjustment) and needing to justify its equivalence to frequentist α-spending for regulatory acceptance.
- **Correct handling path**: ① **Prior selection**: Clarify prior type (informative/weakly-informative/empirical), source and rationale; avoid priors so strong they bias results; sensitivity analysis to test prior robustness; ② **Bayesian stopping boundary**: Preset a posterior-probability-based stopping rule (e.g., P(superiority) > threshold or P(futility) > threshold) and state its frequentist properties; ③ **α-equivalence justification**: Use **simulation (operating characteristics)** to show the Bayesian design's false-positive rate (type I error) under the null is controlled and consistent with the prespecified α — regulators typically require "Bayesian framework but family-wise error rate controlled"; ④ Link with DSMB/interim analysis (§3.4): Bayesian interim still requires preset information disclosure and blinding maintenance; ⑤ **Regulatory communication**: Pre-IND/Type B reach consensus in advance on design and α-equivalence justification, especially adaptive/Bayesian change pre-classification (echoing `ref-ops-gcp-site.md` §3.7).
- **Key points**: Bayesian design does not mean "α control waived" — must use simulation to prove false-positive control and equivalence to prespecified α; priors and stopping rules must be preset, transparent, reproducible.
- ⚠️ Bayesian stopping thresholds, prior conventions, α-equivalence acceptance criteria and simulation requirements are dynamic items — follow current FDA/EMA/NMPA adaptive-design guidelines (officially verify).

### 3.6 Synthetic control construction & bias control (workflow C / B) — difference from historical/external control & regulatory boundary
- **Scenario / Role**: Biostatistician — in a single-arm trial, using a synthetic control (a virtual control arm constructed by modeling historical/external data) to replace a concurrent control, needing to explain construction and bias control.
- **Correct handling path**: ① **Difference from historical/external control**: Synthetic control uses statistical models (e.g., propensity score, Bayesian hierarchical model, mixed-data methods) to "synthesize" multiple historical/external individuals into a virtual control group, which can reduce but **not eliminate** selection bias; ② **Comparable population**: The synthetic control's covariates (tumor type/line/baseline) must be pre-aligned with the trial arm; modeling covariates written into the SAP; ③ **Bias control**: Time trend (concurrency), measurement consistency (RECIST version/BICR), unobserved confounding — use sensitivity analysis/negative-control to test direction; ④ **Transparent reporting**: Preset "synthetic control limitations" section (echoing `ref-ops-gcp-site.md` §3.8 external control 5 points); ⑤ **Regulatory acceptance**: Synthetic-control evidence level is generally lower than concurrent RCT control; must communicate Pre-IND early and use as supportive rather than replacing pivotal evidence.
- **Key points**: Synthetic control is a "modeling-enhanced version" of external control; bias risk remains; construction method and sensitivity must be preset, and it typically serves only as supportive evidence.
- ⚠️ Synthetic-control acceptability and method requirements are dynamic items — follow current CDE/FDA external-control and adaptive-design guidelines (officially verify).

### 3.7 Diagnostic / prognostic / predictive biomarker validation (workflow C) — evidence level analogous to PRO validation
- **Scenario / Role**: Biostatistician / Medical — using diagnostic/prognostic/predictive biomarkers as endpoints or enrichment factors requires validating their analytic/clinical validity.
- **Correct handling path**: ① **Distinguish the three**: Diagnostic (detect disease), prognostic (predict course), predictive (predict treatment effect) — a predictive biomarker must demonstrate interaction with **treatment response**, not mere association; ② **Analytic validation**: Assay accuracy, precision, specificity (linking §4.4/CDISC); ③ **Clinical validation**: Prognostic/predictive value must be validated in an independent cohort, avoiding overfitting; ④ **Regulatory acceptance**: As primary endpoint or enrichment must satisfy the validation framework (analogous to §5.10 PRO validation), preset cutoff and determination; ⑤ Companion diagnostic must follow the synchronized drug-approval path.
- **Key points**: Predictive ≠ prognostic — only demonstrated treatment–biomarker interaction yields enrichment/predictive value; must be independently validated.
- ⚠️ Biomarker validation guidelines, cutoff and companion-diagnostic requirements are dynamic items — follow current FDA/EMA/NMPA biomarker and companion-diagnostic guidelines (officially verify).

### 3.8 Rare-disease Bayesian design & historical-data borrowing (power prior) (workflow C / B) — evidence enhancement under small samples
- **Scenario / Role**: Rare-disease expert / Biostatistics — small-sample rare-disease trials use Bayesian design to borrow historical/external data (power prior) to enhance evidence; must justify the borrowing weight.
- **Correct handling path**: ① **Power prior**: Use historical data as prior, preset a discounting factor controlling the heterogeneity impact of history vs current trial; ② **Borrowing justification**: Must state historical-data comparability (population/design/endpoint); over-borrowing hides current-data risk; ③ **Frequentist properties**: Use simulation to prove Bayesian design false-positive/power controlled (echoing §3.5 α equivalence); ④ **Regulatory communication**: Small-sample rare-disease design requires Pre-IND pre-acceptance; ⑤ Link with §5.12 orphan drug/natural-history external control — historical-data borrowing is the Bayesian form of external control.
- **Key points**: Historical-data borrowing can ease small samples, but the discounting factor must be transparently preset to avoid "borrowing" false precision.
- ⚠️ Power-prior discounting conventions and rare-disease Bayesian acceptability are dynamic items — follow current FDA/EMA/NMPA rare-disease and Bayesian-design guidelines (officially verify).

### 3.9 Sample size re-estimation (SSR) & interim re-estimation (workflow C) — sample size re-estimation under preset rules
- **Scenario / Role**: Biostatistician — during the trial, performing sample size re-estimation (SSR) based on accumulated data must control type I error and be preset.
- **Correct handling path**: ① **Two types of SSR**: Blinded SSR (updating based on estimated parameters such as variance/event rate, not changing the treatment-effect assumption, usually no α penalty) and **efficacy-driven SSR** (must be preset and tied to α-spending/error-rate control; commonly uses an efficacy-blinded design to avoid unblinding bias); ② **Preset**: SSR trigger conditions, timing (information fraction), re-estimation method, upper bound and boundaries must be preset in SAP/protocol; no post-hoc arbitrary adjustment; ③ **Error rate**: Efficacy-driven SSR relying on interim efficacy must use α-spending/adaptive framework to ensure FWER control (echoing §3.3/§3.5); ④ **Communication**: Adaptive SSR requires Pre-IND pre-acceptance; ⑤ **Transparency**: CSR must report the SSR decision and its impact on power (§5.2/§5.3).
- **Key points**: SSR can improve efficiency but requires "preset + error-rate control + transparency"; blinded SSR and efficacy-driven SSR differ in α handling.
- ⚠️ SSR acceptability, α-control method and re-estimation boundaries are dynamic items — follow current FDA/EMA/NMPA adaptive-design and statistical guidelines (officially verify).

### 3.10 DSMB charter & operation (workflow C / D) — governance of the independent data monitoring committee
- **Scenario / Role**: Biostatistics / Sponsor — establish a DSMB/iDMC to oversee safety and efficacy, requiring a charter, independence and a suspension/termination recommendation mechanism (echoing clinical §3.7 iDMC, §3.4 DSMB COI).
- **Correct handling path**: ① **Charter**: Member independence (separated from sponsor operations), operating rules, unblinding procedure, reporting obligations to IRB/sponsor; ② **Operation**: Preset meeting cadence, information supply (blinded/unblinded), decisions (continue/suspend/terminate/modify) and recommendation form; ③ **Suspend/terminate**: DSMB recommendation to suspend/terminate the trial — triggers and execution (echoing §3.4 planned-deviation explanation); ④ **COI**: Member COI disclosure and firewall (§3.4 post-hoc discovery); ⑤ **Regulatory communication**: DSMB recommendations and deviations must be transparently recorded and explained to the regulator (§3.4).
- **Key points**: DSMB value lies in independence and preset rules — charter, independence and written recommendations form checkable governance.
- ⚠️ DSMB charter elements, operating rules and suspend/terminate recommendation acceptability are dynamic items — follow ICH E6(R3) and current DSMB operating standards (officially verify).

### 3.11 RWE study design & bias control (workflow C / registration) — generation of prospective/retrospective RWE
- **Scenario / Role**: RWE / Regulatory — use real-world data to generate evidence (e.g., RWE supporting label, external control, post-marketing); must design the study and control bias (echoing §3.8 external control, §5.16 label expansion).
- **Correct handling path**: ① **Design types**: Retrospective cohort/nested/external-control arm, prospective real-world study, registry, target-trial emulation; ② **Bias control**: Selection bias (PS/MAIC/entropy balancing), confounding (sensitivity analysis/negative control), time trend (concurrent control), measurement consistency; ③ **Data quality**: RWD applicability (completeness/accuracy/timeliness) assessment (echoing §3.8); ④ **Pre-registration**: Protocol pre-registration and prior regulatory acceptance (Pre-IND); ⑤ **Transparency**: Preset limitations section and bias-direction stress testing.
- **Key points**: RWE evidence strength depends on design prespecification and controllable bias — non-preset/non-transparent RWE is not regulator-accepted.
- ⚠️ RWE study-design acceptability, bias-control methods and data-quality requirements are dynamic items — follow current FDA/EMA/NMPA RWE and real-world-study guidelines (officially verify).
